ShieldComplete and ShieldPlatinum are broader American Home Shield plan tiers. The decision is not about choosing a universal winner. It is whether the added premium for the higher tier purchases benefits that are useful for your address, equipment, and budget under the current state-specific agreement.

Plan names, inclusions, prices, limits, promotions, and service fees can change. Request a dated quote and current contract before relying on any comparison.

## Start with identical quote assumptions

Request both plans for the same address, date, billing term, service-fee choice, and optional add-ons. Record:

- Monthly and annual premium
- Enrollment charges
- Service-fee selection
- Promotion and expiration
- Effective date
- Renewal language
- Add-ons
- Cancellation rules

A comparison is unreliable when one quote uses a different service fee or promotion.

## Build a property inventory

List every system and appliance you expect the plan to protect:

- Heating and cooling equipment
- Plumbing and stoppages
- Electrical system
- Water heater
- Refrigerator and freezer
- Dishwasher and disposal
- Oven, range, cooktop, and microwave
- Washer and dryer
- Roof characteristics
- Pools, wells, septic, or other optional equipment

Record quantities, locations, model numbers, condition, known issues, and existing warranties. Compare only benefits that apply to equipment you own.

## Compare the base coverage

Identify what ShieldComplete covers before considering ShieldPlatinum's additions. Check system and appliance categories, component definitions, quantities, and locations.

A broad category such as HVAC or kitchen appliances does not establish coverage for every component. Read definitions and exclusions together.

## Identify the incremental benefits

Create a separate list of benefits that appear only in ShieldPlatinum or have different limits. Do not assign value until you confirm:

1. The benefit appears in the applicable agreement
2. Your property is eligible
3. The relevant item is covered
4. The component and cause qualify
5. The limit is meaningful
6. Local service is available
7. Related work is included

A longer marketing list does not automatically justify an upgrade.

## HVAC comparison

For both tiers, review heating and cooling separately. Check compressors, coils, heat exchangers, blower components, controls, thermostats, refrigerant, line sets, ductwork, matching-system issues, and equipment types.

If ShieldPlatinum shows an enhanced HVAC benefit, determine whether it changes the per-system limit, refrigerant treatment, code coverage, permit coverage, or other specific provision. Compare the exact wording, not a summary label.

A higher HVAC limit can still leave uncovered access, electrical, duct, crane, roof, or compatibility work.

## Appliance comparison

Compare refrigerators, freezers, dishwashers, disposals, cooking appliances, microwaves, washers, dryers, and additional units. Review compressors, sealed systems, pumps, motors, bearings, tubs, drums, controls, sensors, glass, doors, racks, ice makers, and water dispensers.

If the higher tier increases an appliance limit, identify which appliances and expenses it affects. Ask whether diagnosis, labor, parts, tax, shipping, access, and prior claims consume the limit.

## Roof-leak benefit

If roof coverage appears in the higher tier, review it as a separate limited benefit. Confirm:

- Eligible roof types and materials
- Covered leak locations
- Covered causes
- Effective date
- Inspection or maintenance requirements
- Per-event or annual maximum
- Access and diagnosis
- Exclusions for flashing, skylights, chimneys, gutters, structures, or known damage
- Treatment of interior or resulting damage

Limited roof-leak repair is not equivalent to roof replacement or homeowners insurance.

## Code upgrades and permits

A higher tier may advertise support for code or permit expenses. Find the actual limit and covered circumstances. Determine whether the benefit applies to repairs, replacements, or both.

Current-code compliance can require electrical, gas, drainage, ventilation, platform, transition, or safety work. A code benefit may cover only part of the project.

## Access and modifications

Review both plans for access, opening walls or ceilings, excavation, appliance removal, built-in reinstallation, cabinet work, duct transitions, line sets, pads, and utility modifications.

An enhanced equipment benefit may not change these exclusions. Compare the complete installed outcome rather than the price of a component.

## Service-fee choice

AHS may offer different service-fee options. Keep that selection the same while comparing tiers. Then separately evaluate whether another service-fee level improves total cost.

Model:

- No claims
- One covered repair
- Several service requests
- A denied diagnosis
- A repair above a limit
- Replacement or cash settlement

Do not assume a lower service fee is always cheaper; it can be paired with a higher premium.

## Coverage limits

Create a limit table for every important item. Include per-item, component, event, annual, and aggregate maximums.

Ask what counts toward each maximum. A higher-tier limit has value only if the covered failure and related expense would otherwise exceed the lower-tier benefit.

Use current local repair and installed-replacement estimates rather than unsupported national averages.

## Existing conditions

Review definitions of known, unknown, pre-existing, and detectable conditions. An upgrade does not necessarily change these provisions.

Keep inspection reports, service records, maintenance history, and symptom dates. Do not buy the higher tier expecting it to cover a system already failing.

## Maintenance and installation

Compare exclusions for maintenance, improper installation, modifications, rust, corrosion, scale, sediment, pests, blocked airflow, drainage, and utility supply.

Use manufacturer manuals for equipment maintenance. If a claim is denied on one of these grounds, request the diagnosis and cited contract language in writing.

## Contractor process

Plan tier may not change how service is requested or which provider is assigned. Verify local availability for the exact systems and appliances.

Ask whether outside service can be authorized, whether a second opinion is available, and how repeat visits are handled. A broader plan does not guarantee faster dispatch.

## Repair, replacement, and cash settlement

Review who chooses the remedy and how value is calculated. Determine whether ShieldPlatinum changes replacement or settlement rules, not only limits.

Check delivery, haul-away, installation, reconnection, refrigerant, cords, hoses, permits, code work, cabinets, trim, and taxes. An approved replacement can leave substantial uncovered project expense.

## Existing protection

Check manufacturer warranties, credit-card benefits, builder coverage, prior service plans, and homeowners insurance. ShieldPlatinum's added benefit may overlap protection already in place.

Overlap can still offer a different service path, but confirm coordination and reimbursement terms.

## Calculate upgrade value

Start with the annual price difference. Then assign value only to incremental benefits that apply to your property.

Do not count the full stated limit as expected value. Consider the probability of an eligible event qualitatively, the actual component exposure, service fees, exclusions, and the likelihood that related work remains uncovered.

## Example framework without invented prices

Suppose the higher tier adds roof and code benefits and increases selected limits. A property with a recently replaced roof, newer code-compliant equipment, and strong manufacturer warranties may receive little practical value. A property with eligible older systems and meaningful local replacement exposure may find the added provisions more relevant.

This is a framework, not a recommendation for either property.

## Cancellation and renewal

Review whether moving between tiers is allowed, when the change takes effect, and whether a new waiting period applies. Check automatic renewal, notice deadlines, and refund calculations.

At renewal, compare both plans again because terms and pricing may change.

## Decision worksheet

Use columns for:

- Current annual price
- Service fee
- Base covered items
- Incremental ShieldPlatinum benefits
- Item limits
- Exclusions
- Local service
- Installed replacement exposure
- Existing coverage
- Renewal and cancellation

Mark unknown terms and request written clarification.

## Final decision

ShieldPlatinum may be worth the additional cost when its confirmed incremental benefits address significant risks for your property and remain useful after limits and exclusions. ShieldComplete may be sufficient when the added provisions do not apply, overlap existing protection, or do not justify the price difference.

Neither tier is universally better. Compare the current quote and agreement for your property.
