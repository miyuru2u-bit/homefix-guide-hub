Appliance repair costs vary widely. A simple dryer belt repair may be relatively affordable, while a refrigerator compressor problem or wall oven control board failure can cost much more. The appliance type, failed part, labor time, brand, and local service rates all affect the final bill.

Homeowners often want one simple average, but the better approach is to compare costs by appliance type. This guide gives a practical overview of average appliance repair cost by type, explains why costs vary, and helps you decide when repair or replacement makes more sense.

> 💡 **Quick takeaway:** Appliance repair costs are usually driven by diagnosis, labor, parts, appliance complexity, and location. Refrigerators, built-in appliances, front-load washers, wall ovens, and premium cooking appliances often cost more to repair than basic dryers or simple ranges.

## What is included in appliance repair cost?

An appliance repair bill may include several charges:

- Diagnostic fee
- Service call fee
- Labor
- Parts
- Return visit
- Emergency or weekend surcharge
- Trip charge
- Disposal of old parts
- Taxes
- Additional installation or access labor

Some companies apply the diagnostic fee toward the repair if you approve the work. Others charge it separately. Always ask whether the estimate includes parts, labor, return visits, and fees.

## Why repair costs vary so much

Two homeowners can have the same appliance problem and pay different amounts because repair cost depends on: appliance brand, appliance age, model complexity, part availability, local labor rates, technician experience, built-in vs freestanding installation, warranty coverage, emergency timing, whether multiple parts failed, and whether the first diagnosis is correct.

A premium appliance may cost more to repair than a basic appliance, even for a similar symptom.

## Refrigerator repair cost

Refrigerator repairs are often among the most expensive appliance repairs because refrigerators run constantly and use complex cooling systems.

Common repairs include the evaporator fan, condenser fan, defrost heater, defrost thermostat, temperature sensor, control board, door gasket, ice maker, water inlet valve, compressor, and sealed-system components.

Refrigerator repair costs tend to be higher for French door, built-in, counter-depth, smart, ice-maker, and water-dispenser refrigerators, as well as premium brands.

Related guide: [Refrigerator Repair Cost Guide](/blog/refrigerator-repair-cost-guide)

## Dishwasher repair cost

Common dishwasher repairs include the drain pump, circulation pump, motor, water inlet valve, door latch, door seal, float switch, heating element, control board, leak sensor, and drain hose.

Minor drainage or latch problems may be less costly. Pump, motor, control board, and leak repairs can be more expensive. Cost may also increase if the unit is panel-ready, built into custom cabinetry, or difficult to access.

Related guide: [Dishwasher Repair Cost Guide](/blog/dishwasher-repair-cost-guide)

## Washer repair cost

Common washer repairs include the drain pump, lid switch, door lock, water inlet valve, belt, motor, control board, pressure switch, shock absorbers, bearings, and tub or drum assembly.

Front-load washers and high-efficiency models may cost more to repair than basic top-load washers, especially if the issue involves bearings, drum components, electronics, or access in a stacked installation.

Related guide: [Washing Machine Repair Cost Guide](/blog/washing-machine-repair-cost-guide)

## Dryer repair cost

Dryers are often more repair-friendly than many other appliances, especially basic electric models.

Common repairs include the thermal fuse, heating element, thermostat, belt, idler pulley, drum rollers, motor, door switch, moisture sensor, control board, and gas valve coils.

Dryer repair cost can increase if the dryer is gas, stacked, smart, or installed in a tight space.

Related guide: [Dryer Repair Cost Guide](/blog/dryer-repair-cost-guide)

## Oven repair cost

Common oven repairs include the bake element, broil element, igniter, temperature sensor, control board, touch panel, door lock, convection fan, relay board, and wiring.

Simple heating element or igniter repairs may be reasonable. Control board, touch panel, and built-in wall oven repairs may cost more. Wall ovens can be more expensive because the unit may need to be pulled from cabinetry.

Related guide: [Oven & Stove Repair Cost Guide](/blog/oven-stove-repair-cost-guide)

## Range and cooktop repair cost

Common repairs include burner elements, gas igniters, spark modules, control knobs, infinite switches, temperature sensors, control boards, gas valves, induction modules, and glass cooktop surfaces.

Basic gas or electric ranges may be economical to repair. Induction cooktops, premium ranges, dual-fuel models, and smart ranges may cost more. Glass cooktop damage can be expensive and may not be covered by warranty if it is accidental.

## Microwave repair cost

Countertop microwaves are often replaced rather than repaired because replacement is usually affordable. Built-in microwaves, microwave drawers, and combination microwave-wall oven units may be worth repairing because replacement and installation are more expensive.

Common microwave repairs include the door switch, magnetron, diode, capacitor, control board, touchpad, turntable motor, and thermal fuse. Microwave repairs involving high-voltage components should always be handled by qualified professionals.

## Garbage disposal repair cost

Garbage disposals are often replaced rather than repaired if the motor fails or the unit leaks from the body. Simple jams may be inexpensive to address. Older leaking disposals are often replaced.

## Freezer repair cost

Common freezer repairs include the thermostat, evaporator fan, compressor, start relay, defrost heater, door gasket, control board, and sealed-system components. A major sealed-system repair on an older freezer may not be worth it unless the unit is large, premium, or built-in.

## Built-in appliance repair cost

Built-in appliances often cost more to repair because access takes time — examples include built-in refrigerators, wall ovens, panel-ready dishwashers, microwave drawers, built-in wine coolers, and downdraft cooktops.

Added costs may involve removing the appliance, protecting cabinetry, disconnecting utilities, reinstalling the appliance, working around custom panels, and matching replacement dimensions.

## Most expensive appliance repairs by type

Some repairs are especially likely to be expensive: refrigerator compressor, refrigerator sealed-system repair, built-in refrigerator repair, washer bearing repair, washer tub assembly repair, washer motor replacement, wall oven control board, induction cooktop module, premium range control system, dishwasher circulation pump, and built-in microwave control repair.

## Lower-cost appliance repairs

Some repairs are often more affordable: dryer belt, dryer thermal fuse, oven igniter, oven heating element, dishwasher door latch, washer lid switch, refrigerator door gasket, basic thermostat, simple switch, and water inlet valve.

## Diagnostic and labor fees

Ask: What is the diagnostic fee? Is it applied to the repair? Is there a trip charge? Does the fee change for built-in appliances? Is the fee higher for emergency service? What happens if parts are unavailable?

Labor cost depends on repair complexity, technician skill, local wage rates, time required, appliance access, disassembly needed, testing required, safety risks, and whether a second visit is needed. Labor is often a major part of the final bill.

## Warranty coverage and repair cost

A warranty can reduce repair cost if the issue is covered. Coverage may come from a manufacturer warranty, extended appliance warranty, retailer protection plan, home warranty, or credit card benefit. But warranties may include service fees, deductibles, claim limits, exclusions, required technician networks, and approval delays.

Related guide: [What Does an Appliance Warranty Cover?](/blog/what-does-an-appliance-warranty-cover)

## When appliance repair is worth it

Repair is more likely worth it when the appliance is newer, repair cost is modest, the appliance has been reliable, parts are available, repair is covered by warranty, replacement would be expensive, the appliance is built-in or premium, or the repair solves a clear isolated problem.

## When replacement is better

Replacement may be better when repair cost is close to half of replacement, the appliance is near end of expected life, parts are discontinued, repairs are frequent, energy efficiency is poor, the appliance no longer meets your needs, the repair estimate is uncertain, or warranty coverage is not available.

## How to avoid overpaying

Get a clear written estimate, ask whether diagnosis is included, confirm parts and labor, ask about return visit fees, compare repair to replacement cost, check warranty coverage first, look up appliance age, ask if parts are available, get a second opinion for major repairs, and avoid approving repair under pressure.

## Final recommendation

Average appliance repair cost depends heavily on appliance type. Refrigerators, built-in appliances, front-load washers, wall ovens, induction cooktops, and premium ranges often cost more to repair. Basic dryers, simple ranges, and minor component repairs are often more manageable.

Before approving a repair, compare the estimate with the appliance's age, replacement cost, parts availability, and warranty coverage.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the average appliance repair cost?**
Many appliance repairs fall somewhere between a modest service call and several hundred dollars, but the exact cost depends on the appliance type, part, labor rate, brand, age, and location.

**Which appliance usually costs the most to repair?**
Refrigerators, built-in appliances, front-load washers, wall ovens, induction cooktops, and premium ranges are often among the more expensive appliances to repair.

**What affects appliance repair cost the most?**
The biggest cost factors are diagnostic fees, labor time, part cost, appliance brand, built-in installation, local labor rates, emergency service, and whether parts are available.

**Is it worth repairing an appliance?**
Repair may be worth it if the appliance is newer, reliable, and the repair cost is well below replacement cost. Replacement may be better if the appliance is old or the repair is close to half the cost of a new one.

**Do warranties cover appliance repair costs?**
Manufacturer warranties, extended warranties, home warranties, and credit card benefits may cover some repair costs, but service fees, exclusions, and claim limits can apply.
