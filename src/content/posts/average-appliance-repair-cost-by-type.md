Appliance type is only a starting point. Two refrigerators can have very different repair bills when one needs a gasket and the other requires sealed-system work.

## What a complete estimate should show

- Diagnostic or trip charge
- Labor and estimated time
- Part number, price, and availability
- Shipping or return-visit charges
- Access, removal, or reinstallation work
- Taxes and disposal
- Repair warranty
- Total due if you decline the repair

## Compare like with like

Ask whether the estimate uses original, aftermarket, refurbished, or customer-supplied parts. Confirm whether diagnosis is credited toward approved work and whether a second trip is included.

## Appliance-specific variables

Refrigeration can involve sealed-system and refrigerant work. Laundry costs vary with bearings, tubs, motors, pumps, controls, and stacked access. Dishwashers combine cabinetry, plumbing, electrical, pumps, and leak diagnosis. Cooking equipment may involve gas, high voltage, built-in removal, glass, controls, or induction electronics.

## Repair or replacement

Do not use a fixed percentage rule alone. Compare the written repair estimate with the installed cost of a suitable replacement and consider repair history, downtime, efficiency, fit, and parts support.
