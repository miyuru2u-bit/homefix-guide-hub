When an appliance fails, compare three paths: a warranty claim, direct repair, and replacement. No fixed percentage or price threshold works for every appliance.

## Decision worksheet

| Factor | Warranty claim | Direct repair | Replacement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate cost | Service fee plus uncovered charges | Diagnosis, parts, and labor | Product, delivery, installation, and disposal |
| Timing | Authorization and assigned-service process | Local availability | Product and installer availability |
| Control | Contract determines provider and remedy | Greater technician choice | Full model choice |
| Risk | Denial, limit, or partial settlement | Additional damage or repeat repair | Higher upfront cost and installation issues |

## Warranty math

Estimate:

**service fee + excluded work + amount above the limit + delay or inconvenience cost**

Then compare that with written repair and installed-replacement estimates. Do not count the entire annual premium as a cost of one claim if you already bought the plan, but include it when deciding whether to renew.

## Repairability

Consider the diagnosis, appliance age, condition, previous repairs, parts availability, installation complexity, and expected useful life after repair. A percentage-of-replacement rule is only a prompt, not a decision standard.

## Authorization

Before arranging direct service, check whether doing so would forfeit reimbursement. For urgent problems, document the condition, mitigate damage safely, and follow the contract's emergency procedure.
