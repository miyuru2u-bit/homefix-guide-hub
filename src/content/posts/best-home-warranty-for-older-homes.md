Older homes are exactly where a home warranty earns its keep. A 20- or 40-year-old house often has original HVAC, aging water heaters, and appliances well past their prime — the kind of equipment that fails expensively and without warning. The good news: most home warranties cover items regardless of age. The catch: not all providers handle older equipment equally, and coverage caps, pre-existing terms, and add-ons matter far more for an older home than a new build.

This guide ranks the best home warranties for older homes and explains exactly what to look for so a single big repair doesn't blow up your budget.

## What matters most for an older home

Before comparing providers, understand the three factors that make or break coverage on an aging house:

**Coverage caps.** Older systems fail in big, expensive ways (think a full HVAC replacement). A plan with low per-item caps can leave you paying thousands out of pocket. Prioritize providers with high caps.

**Age and maintenance rules.** The best providers cover items regardless of age and don't demand maintenance records. Some stricter contracts can deny claims if you can't prove upkeep — a real risk on a home you've owned for decades or just bought.

**Pre-existing condition terms.** Almost every provider excludes known pre-existing conditions. Only a few are forgiving about unknown ones — which is common in older homes where you can't verify every system's history.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you just bought an older home, you likely don't know the full repair history of its systems. That makes Cinch's coverage of unknown pre-existing conditions unusually valuable — it's one of the few providers that won't automatically deny a claim just because the issue predates your purchase.

## 1. American Home Shield — best overall for older homes

American Home Shield (AHS) is the category veteran and the strongest all-around pick for older homes for three reasons: it covers items regardless of age, brand, or maintenance history, it offers some of the highest coverage caps in the industry (especially for systems like HVAC), and it provides plan flexibility via its three tiers (ShieldSilver, ShieldGold, ShieldPlatinum) and selectable service fee.

For an older home, the high caps are the headline. When a 25-year-old furnace or air conditioner finally dies, AHS's higher payout limits mean more of that repair or replacement is covered. Pricing starts around $29.99/month for systems-only ShieldSilver and rises with coverage depth. The trade-off is a mixed customer-service reputation and a high complaint volume (proportional to its huge customer base).

## 2. Cinch Home Services — best for forgiving terms

Cinch is the standout for homeowners worried about their older home's murky history. It covers some unknown pre-existing conditions — those that couldn't be spotted by a simple visual inspection or test — and includes coverage for rust and corrosion damage, both rare in this industry and both common problems in aging plumbing and systems. Cinch also offers a 180-day workmanship guarantee, one of the longest available, so re-repairs within six months are covered.

Plans typically run $40–$70/month with a higher service fee ($100–$150). The forgiving terms make Cinch especially appealing if you've recently purchased an older home and can't verify the condition of every system.

## 3. Liberty Home Guard — best for customization

Older homes often have unusual extras — well pumps, septic systems, older pool equipment, second refrigerators — that standard plans skip. Liberty Home Guard offers 40+ add-ons, by far the widest selection, letting you tailor coverage to your specific older home. It also posts among the highest claim-approval rates and strong customer-satisfaction scores in independent surveys, though it has a shorter track record than AHS. Plans run roughly $49–$60/month.

## 4 & 5. Choice and Select — budget options (mind the caps)

If price is the priority, Choice Home Warranty ($46–$60/month, flat ~$85 service fee, large contractor network) and Select Home Warranty ($35–$48/month, free limited roof-leak coverage) are the budget picks. Both cover items regardless of age, but their lower coverage caps are a real concern for older homes — a major HVAC failure may only be partially covered. They're best when you're protecting an older home with mostly mid-value equipment rather than expensive, failure-prone systems.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** The cheapest plan can be the most expensive choice for an older home. If a $35/month plan caps an HVAC repair at a few hundred dollars, you'll cover the rest yourself. Always compare per-item coverage caps, not just the monthly premium.

## How to choose for your older home

- **Expensive, aging systems (HVAC, water heater):** Choose American Home Shield for its high caps.
- **Recently bought, unknown history:** Choose Cinch for forgiving pre-existing and rust/corrosion terms.
- **Unusual extras to cover:** Choose Liberty Home Guard for its add-on catalog.
- **Tight budget, mid-value equipment:** Consider Choice or Select, but verify the caps.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do home warranties cover old appliances and systems?**
Yes. Most home warranties cover items regardless of age, as long as the breakdown is due to normal wear and tear and the item was properly maintained. Age itself is not a disqualifier — pre-existing conditions and neglect are.

**What is the best home warranty for an older home?**
American Home Shield is the most common top pick because it covers items regardless of age, doesn't require maintenance records, and offers high coverage caps. Cinch is a strong alternative thanks to its coverage of some unknown pre-existing conditions and rust/corrosion.

**Does an older home cost more to cover?**
Usually not. Most providers price plans by coverage level rather than home age, so an older home typically pays the same premium as a newer one — which makes warranties especially good value for aging properties.

**Does a home warranty cover pre-existing conditions in an old house?**
Generally no. Known pre-existing conditions are excluded by nearly all providers. The exception is Cinch, which covers some unknown pre-existing conditions that couldn't be detected by a simple inspection.

**Are home warranties worth it for older homes?**
Often yes. Older homes have aging systems and appliances more likely to fail, so a warranty can offset large repair bills and make costs predictable — provided you choose a plan with high enough coverage caps.
