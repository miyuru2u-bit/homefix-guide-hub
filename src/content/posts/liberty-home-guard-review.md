Liberty Home Guard sells service-contract coverage for appliances, home systems, and optional property features. Its customization is worth evaluating, but add-on counts and online ratings should not substitute for contract review.

## Quick verdict

Liberty may suit a property with nonstandard features that are eligible for optional coverage. The best comparison is the price and limit for each needed add-on—not the size of the catalog or an unsupported claim about approval rates.

## What to verify

- Current base plans and covered components
- Add-ons available at your address
- Premium, service fee, and renewal terms
- Limits for base coverage and each add-on
- Waiting period and cancellation terms
- Contractor authorization rules
- Exclusions for maintenance and pre-existing conditions

## Customer ratings

Ratings can be useful for spotting recurring service themes, but they vary by platform and change over time. Review recent positive and negative reports, the company's responses, and complaint resolution—not only an average score.

## Add-on economics

Only add coverage for an item you own and could not comfortably repair or replace. Compare the annual add-on premium, service fee, and maximum payout with the likely repair cost.

## Who should consider it

Liberty may be worth comparing for homes with pools, pumps, or other eligible extras. It may be less attractive if multiple add-ons make the annual cost approach the amount you could reserve for repairs.
