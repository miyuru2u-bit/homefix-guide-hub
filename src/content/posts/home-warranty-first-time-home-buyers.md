First-time buyers should treat a home warranty as one possible risk-management tool, not a substitute for inspection, insurance, maintenance, or emergency savings.

## Buyer checklist

- Review inspection findings and seller disclosures
- Record system and appliance ages and known defects
- Identify manufacturer, builder, retailer, or credit-card coverage
- Compare the annual premium and service fees
- Check item-level and aggregate limits
- Read maintenance and existing-condition exclusions
- Confirm effective dates and waiting periods
- Understand contractor authorization and cash-out rules
- Compare the plan with self-funding likely repairs

## Warranty versus insurance

A home warranty is usually a service contract for specifically listed items and causes of failure. Homeowners insurance is a separate policy with different covered events, deductibles, exclusions, and lender considerations. Read both documents.

## Seller-funded plans

If a seller offers a plan or contribution, evaluate it as carefully as a buyer-funded product. Confirm the provider, product, effective date, limits, and renewal terms rather than assuming that a zero upfront cost means strong coverage.

> Preserve the complete agreement, quote, inspection, and proof of existing warranties.
