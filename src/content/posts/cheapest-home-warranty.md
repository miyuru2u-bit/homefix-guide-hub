Everyone wants to pay less for home warranty coverage, but the cheapest plan isn't automatically the best deal. A low premium paired with low coverage caps can leave you covering most of a big repair yourself — defeating the purpose. The goal is the cheapest plan that still pays out enough when something expensive breaks. This guide ranks the genuinely worthwhile budget options and shows how to cut costs without gutting your protection.

## The cheap-plan trap

The reason some plans are so cheap is simple: the savings come from somewhere. Usually it's one (or more) of these:

- **Lower coverage caps** — the plan pays less per repair, so you cover the rest.
- **Fewer included items** — the base plan skips things like AC or refrigerators.
- **Higher service fee** — a low premium paired with a high per-visit fee.

None of these make a plan "bad" — they make it cheaper. The trick is making sure the trade-offs match your situation. A low-cap plan is fine for newer, mid-value appliances but risky for an aging HVAC system.

> 💡 **Tip:** The smartest way to lower your premium without losing protection is to choose a higher service-call fee. Providers like AHS let you trade a higher per-visit fee for a lower monthly cost. If you don't expect many claims, this saves money all year while keeping your coverage caps intact.

## 1. Select Home Warranty — lowest premium

Select is the budget champion, with plans frequently $35–$48/month and regular promotions adding free months. It even includes limited free roof-leak coverage, which most rivals charge extra for. The trade-off is lower coverage caps, so it's best when you're protecting newer or mid-value equipment rather than an expensive, failure-prone system. For pure low cost, nothing beats it.

## 2. American Home Shield ShieldSilver — cheapest systems-only

If your main concern is systems (HVAC, plumbing, electrical) rather than appliances, AHS's ShieldSilver plan starts around $29.99/month — one of the lowest entry prices anywhere — while still carrying AHS's high systems caps. That combination of low price and high caps makes it arguably the best cheap plan for homeowners worried about a big system failure. You can also tune the premium via the service fee.

## 3. Choice Home Warranty — best value-to-coverage

Choice isn't the absolute cheapest, but at $46–$60/month with a flat ~$85 service fee, moderate caps, and a large contractor network, it offers the best balance of low cost and real coverage. For most budget shoppers who want both appliances and systems covered, Choice's Total plan is the sweet spot.

## 4 & 5. Liberty Home Guard and Cinch

Liberty Home Guard ($49–$60/mo) is slightly more but pairs affordability with the best claim-approval and service reputation — worth the few extra dollars to avoid denial headaches. Cinch ($40–$70/mo) brings forgiving terms (rust/corrosion, some unknown pre-existing conditions) into a mid-budget price, good value for older homes despite a higher service fee.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** A plan that's cheap because of low caps can be the most expensive choice when a major system fails. Before buying any budget plan, find the per-item cap for your most expensive equipment (usually HVAC) and make sure it's high enough to matter. The cheapest premium is a false economy if the payout won't cover a real repair.

## How to get the cheapest plan that's worth it

- **Lowest possible premium, mid-value equipment:** Select Home Warranty.
- **Cheapest systems coverage with high caps:** AHS ShieldSilver.
- **Best overall value:** Choice Home Warranty.
- **Cheap but want great service:** Liberty Home Guard.

Other money-savers: pay annually instead of monthly, choose a higher service fee, pick a single-category plan (appliances-only or systems-only) if you don't need both, and watch for promotional free months.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the cheapest home warranty?**
Select Home Warranty is consistently among the cheapest, with plans often in the $35–$48/month range. American Home Shield's systems-only ShieldSilver can also start around $29.99/month, making it one of the lowest entry prices for systems coverage.

**Are cheap home warranties worth it?**
They can be — if the coverage caps are high enough for your equipment. The risk with cheap plans is low per-item caps that leave you paying the difference on a big repair. Always compare caps, not just premiums.

**Why are some home warranties so cheap?**
Lower premiums usually mean lower coverage caps, fewer included items, or a higher service fee. The savings come from somewhere, so read the contract to see what's been trimmed.

**How can I lower my home warranty premium?**
Choose a higher service-call fee (which lowers your monthly cost), pick a systems-only or appliances-only plan instead of a combined one, pay annually, and watch for promotional free months.

**What is the cheapest home warranty with good coverage?**
Choice Home Warranty and American Home Shield's ShieldSilver offer a strong balance of low price and adequate caps. Select is cheaper still but with lower caps — fine for mid-value equipment.
