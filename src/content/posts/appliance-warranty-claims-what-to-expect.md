The warranty contract—not a general checklist—controls an appliance claim. Read it before hiring a technician or disassembling the appliance.

## Before filing

- Confirm the coverage and expiration dates
- Match the appliance, model, and serial number
- Identify the covered parts and causes of failure
- Review exclusions and payout limits
- Check whether prior authorization is required
- Confirm service fees and whether they are refundable
- Save receipts, installation records, maintenance records, photos, and videos

## During the claim

Describe symptoms rather than guessing the failed part. Record the claim number, dates, representative names, technician findings, approvals, and promised next steps. Ask whether additional work requires separate authorization.

## Repair or replacement

An unrepairable appliance does not automatically produce a new equivalent model. The contract may allow another repair attempt, a selected replacement, store credit, reimbursement, depreciated value, or a cash settlement subject to limits. Check delivery, installation, modifications, permits, and haul-away separately.

## If coverage is denied

Request the decision and cited contract language in writing. Compare it with the diagnosis and your records, then follow the stated appeal or complaint procedure. Preserve all correspondence.

> For gas odors, fire, electrical hazards, or flooding, prioritize emergency safety procedures rather than waiting for warranty authorization.
