Washing machines fail differently from refrigerators. A fridge gives you 24 hours of grace; a broken washer is just a pile of wet laundry. That urgency leads many homeowners to overspend on either repair or replacement. Here's how to think about it cleanly.

## What typically breaks

The failure pattern is well documented across appliance service records:

- **Years 1–5:** Mostly electronic — control boards, sensors, door locks. Often covered by manufacturer warranty.
- **Years 6–10:** Pumps, belts, and water inlet valves. Mid-range repairs ($150–$300).
- **Years 10+:** Bearings, transmissions, motors. These are the expensive failures that drive replacement decisions.

## DIY checks before you call

A few things are genuinely safe to check yourself:

1. **Clean the drain pump filter.** On front-loaders, there's usually a small access panel at the bottom front. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of "my washer won't drain."
2. **Check the hoses.** Kinked or split inlet hoses cause leaks blamed on the machine.
3. **Reset the cycle.** Unplug the machine for 60 seconds. Modern boards sometimes lock up and a full power-cycle clears the error.
4. **Run an empty hot cycle with washer cleaner.** Bad odors and slow cycles are often biofilm, not a mechanical issue.

> **Safety note:** Disconnect both power and the water supply before opening any panel. Front-load drums hold residual water even when "empty," and 120V components inside the cabinet are live when plugged in.

## When to call a pro

Get a quote from a technician if you notice:

- Loud banging or grinding during the spin cycle (likely bearings)
- Water under the machine that isn't from the hose connections
- An error code that recurs after a power cycle
- The drum doesn't turn, even though the motor sounds like it's running

## Repair vs replace: the decision framework

Use these thresholds:

- **Under 6 years old:** Repair almost any failure under $500.
- **6–8 years old:** Repair if the quote is under $400, or under 40% of a replacement.
- **9+ years old:** Only repair sub-$250 issues. Bigger failures mean replacement is the better long-term value.
- **Drum bearings, transmission, or motor failure on a unit past warranty:** Replace. These repairs are expensive and the next big failure is usually within 18 months.

## Warranty coverage notes

Home warranty plans typically cover washer mechanical and electrical failures for a $75–$125 service fee. **Common exclusions:**

- Hoses (considered consumables)
- Damage from overloading or unbalanced loads
- Water-inlet valve failures caused by sediment if you don't have a whole-home water filter
- Cosmetic damage to the cabinet or control panel

Manufacturer warranties usually cover the motor and transmission for 5–10 years separately from the 1-year full-unit warranty. If your machine is in that window, check before paying anyone — many homeowners pay out of pocket for repairs that are still covered.
