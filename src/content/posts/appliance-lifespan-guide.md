Published lifespan figures describe populations, not the remaining life of one appliance. Use age as one input rather than a verdict.

## Remaining-life checklist

1. Record the full model and serial number.
2. Confirm installation and purchase dates if available.
3. Review usage intensity and environment.
4. Check maintenance and repair history.
5. Diagnose current symptoms before estimating life.
6. Check parts and local service availability.
7. Compare current energy use and performance.
8. Price a suitable installed replacement.

## Category-specific factors

For refrigerators, inspect temperatures, seals, airflow, coils, and sealed-system history. For washers, consider leaks, bearings, suspension, pumps, and controls. For dryers, inspect venting and heating performance. For dishwashers, assess leaks, pumps, racks, and cleaning performance. For ovens and ranges, consider controls, heating, ignition, glass, gas, and built-in access.

## Maintenance

Use the manufacturer manual. Common tasks include cleaning filters and vents, maintaining airflow, avoiding overload, checking water and gas connections, and addressing leaks or unusual heat promptly.

## Planning replacements

Keep a household equipment list with dates, warranties, maintenance, repairs, and replacement priorities. This supports budgeting without treating a generic age range as a countdown.
