Select Home Warranty and Choice Home Warranty are often considered by shoppers seeking lower-cost service-contract options. Neither provider is universally cheaper or better. An advertised premium, promotional discount, or roof benefit can distract from service fees, limits, exclusions, renewal cost, and local contractor availability.

Compare current written quotes and state-specific agreements for the same address, date, items, billing term, and service-fee assumptions.

## Build a property inventory

List:

- Heating and cooling equipment
- Plumbing and electrical systems
- Water heaters
- Refrigerators and freezers
- Dishwashers and disposals
- Ovens, ranges, cooktops, and microwaves
- Washers and dryers
- Roof type and known condition
- Pools, wells, septic, or other optional items

Record quantities, locations, model numbers, condition, known problems, and existing warranties. Use the same inventory for both companies.

## Obtain current documents

Collect a dated quote, complete agreement, add-on terms, service-fee information, promotion conditions, renewal language, and cancellation rules from Select and Choice.

Save the exact documents. Plan names, prices, limits, and offers can change.

## Compare equivalent plans

Do not compare plans solely by name or tier. Match covered systems, appliances, components, and add-ons. If one plan includes an item the other omits, add the corresponding option or record the difference.

Create one row per item and columns for Select, Choice, limits, exclusions, effective dates, contractor process, and remedy.

## First-term price

Record premium, billing schedule, enrollment charges, add-ons, service fee, and promotion. Convert monthly and annual quotes to the same period.

Check whether free months, discounts, or bundled options require annual payment, automatic renewal, or other conditions. A lower first payment is not necessarily a lower contract obligation.

## Renewal price

Ask how renewal and price changes are handled. A promotion may apply only to the first term.

Set a reminder before renewal and obtain new agreements. Do not base a long-term decision on an introductory quote alone.

## Service fees

Clarify whether the service fee applies per request, visit, appliance, system, trade, or issue. Ask about denied diagnoses, reassignment, callbacks, and repeat visits.

A lower premium paired with a higher service fee can produce different results depending on claim count. Test several scenarios instead of assuming one structure is cheaper.

## Covered systems

For HVAC, compare equipment types, compressors, coils, heat exchangers, refrigerant, controls, thermostats, ductwork, matching issues, access, permits, and code upgrades.

For plumbing, electrical, and water heating, review stoppages, pipe and wiring locations, panels, fixtures, pumps, excavation, opening surfaces, and restoration.

A broad systems label does not establish complete coverage.

## Covered appliances

Confirm refrigerators, additional refrigeration, dishwashers, disposals, cooking appliances, microwaves, washers, dryers, and specialty units.

Compare compressors, sealed systems, pumps, motors, bearings, tubs, drums, controls, sensors, glass, racks, doors, handles, hoses, trim, and accessories.

## Roof-leak provisions

If Select or Choice offers roof-related coverage, compare it separately. Confirm:

1. Eligible roof types and materials
2. Covered leak locations
3. Covered causes
4. Effective date
5. Inspection or maintenance requirements
6. Per-event and annual limits
7. Access and diagnosis
8. Excluded flashing, skylights, chimneys, gutters, structures, or known damage
9. Interior or resulting-damage treatment
10. Repair or settlement method

Limited leak coverage is not the same as full roof replacement or homeowners insurance.

## Coverage limits

Find per-component, per-item, per-system, per-event, annual, and aggregate limits. Add-ons and roof benefits may have separate maximums.

Ask whether diagnosis, labor, parts, shipping, refrigerant, tax, access, and previous claims reduce the limit. Compare usable benefits with current local repair and installed-replacement estimates.

Do not identify a limit winner from one headline figure.

## Effective dates

Record purchase, issue, effective, waiting-period, add-on, roof, expiration, and renewal dates. Obtain any exception in writing.

A newly purchased contract should not be assumed to cover a problem already present. Review existing-condition language.

## Existing conditions

Compare definitions of known, unknown, pre-existing, and detectable conditions. Keep inspection reports, disclosures, maintenance history, photographs, and prior repair invoices.

If a claim is denied, request the diagnosis and cited provision. Documentation clarifies timing but does not guarantee approval.

## Maintenance and installation

Review both contracts for maintenance, improper installation, modification, blocked airflow, drainage, utility supply, rust, corrosion, scale, sediment, pests, weather, power events, and unauthorized repair.

Use manufacturer instructions for equipment. For roofs, obtain qualified inspection and follow local requirements.

## Contractor process

Learn how each company receives service requests, assigns contractors, and authorizes outside work. Verify local availability for the brands and equipment at the address.

Ask:

- What happens if no contractor accepts the request?
- Can another provider be assigned?
- Is outside service possible with authorization?
- Is a second opinion available?
- Who pays diagnosis after denial?
- How are repeat visits handled?

Network-size and dispatch-speed claims should not replace local verification.

## Repair, replacement, and cash settlement

Compare who chooses the remedy and how value is calculated. Either provider may retain rights to repair, replace, reimburse, or settle in cash under its agreement.

Review delivery, haul-away, installation, refrigerant, lines, ducts, cords, hoses, trim, panels, cabinets, permits, code upgrades, and taxes. An approved benefit may not cover the complete installed project.

## Cancellation

Compare cancellation windows, required notice, fees, claims deductions, refund formulas, and the effect of promotions. Retain written confirmation after cancellation.

A lower premium can be offset by restrictive or poorly understood cancellation terms.

## Customer reports

Reviews can identify questions about communication, contractor scheduling, roof claims, promotions, claim interpretation, and cancellation. Ratings reflect different locations, contracts, dates, and customer populations.

Use detailed current reports as research leads. Do not convert them into an unsupported reputation score or likely approval outcome.

## Scenario analysis

Run the same scenarios for both providers:

- No claims
- One covered appliance repair
- One covered system repair
- A denied diagnosis
- Several service requests
- A roof leak
- A failure above the limit
- Unavailable parts
- Replacement or cash settlement
- No local contractor

Record total cost, timing, control, and likely remedy.

## When Select may fit

Select may merit consideration when its current quote, promotions, covered items, limits, roof terms, and local service align with the property.

A low advertised premium or roof benefit alone does not establish value.

## When Choice may fit

Choice may merit consideration when its current plan structure, fees, limits, and service process better match the equipment and household priorities.

That conclusion also requires current local-service verification.

## Compare self-funding

A repair reserve preserves unused funds and contractor choice but leaves the household responsible for full costs and coordination. Both service contracts transfer selected risks only.

Compare Select, Choice, and self-funding over the same period.

## Comparison worksheet

Record:

- Quote date and address
- Plan and premium
- Billing term and promotion
- Service fee
- Effective dates
- Covered items and components
- Limits
- Roof terms
- Exclusions
- Local service
- Repair and settlement method
- Renewal and cancellation

Attach the current agreements and mark unknowns.

## Final decision

Select and Choice should be treated as candidates until their current quotes, contracts, and local service are compared. Choose the agreement that provides useful coverage for the property's actual risks at an acceptable total cost.

Do not decide from a promotional price, roof headline, budget ranking, network claim, or reputation score alone.
