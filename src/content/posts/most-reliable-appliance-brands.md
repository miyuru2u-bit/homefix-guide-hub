Brand-level rankings can hide large differences between appliance categories, factories, model generations, and feature packages. A useful reliability decision is model-specific and local.

## Research framework

1. **Exact model:** Search the complete model number, not only the brand.
2. **Recall history:** Check the CPSC database and manufacturer notices.
3. **Warranty:** Compare parts, labor, sealed-system, motor, and registration terms.
4. **Parts:** Check availability and expected support life for common components.
5. **Local service:** Confirm authorized and independent technicians nearby.
6. **Repairability:** Consider access, proprietary tools, and modular components.
7. **Owner patterns:** Look for repeated, model-specific failures across several sources.
8. **Ownership cost:** Include purchase, energy, maintenance, repair, and replacement costs.

## Category-specific checks

### Refrigerators

Review compressor and sealed-system terms, ice-maker complexity, water components, board availability, and service access.

### Dishwashers

Review pump, heating, leak-protection, rack, filter, and control-system reports.

### Washers and dryers

Check bearings, seals, drain pumps, heating systems, venting needs, motors, and controls. Installation and maintenance strongly affect outcomes.

### Ranges and ovens

Check igniters, elements, temperature controls, boards, induction modules, and local availability of qualified service.

## Interpreting reviews

Owner reviews are not controlled reliability studies. Separate shipping and installation complaints from product failures, check purchase dates, and look for recurring symptoms tied to the exact model.

## Practical decision

Prefer a proven model with adequate local service and accessible parts over a brand reputation unsupported by model-level evidence. Buy only features you expect to use and follow installation and maintenance instructions.
