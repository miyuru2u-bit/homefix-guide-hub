Choice Home Warranty and American Home Shield both sell service contracts for selected home systems and appliances. Neither provider is universally better or cheaper. The useful comparison starts with current written quotes and state-specific agreements for the same property, date, billing term, and coverage.

Marketing pages can emphasize different strengths, but contract language controls claim eligibility, limits, service, and remedies.

## Use equivalent quote assumptions

Request both quotes on the same day and use:

- The same property address
- The same occupancy type
- Equivalent systems and appliance coverage
- The same optional items
- Comparable billing terms
- Comparable service-fee assumptions

Record promotion conditions, effective dates, and renewal language. A price comparison is not valid when one quote omits important coverage or uses a different fee structure.

## Build a property inventory

List the home's HVAC, plumbing, electrical, water-heating, kitchen, and laundry equipment. Include quantities, locations, model numbers, condition, known issues, and existing warranties.

Use the inventory to check both agreements line by line. Do not give a provider credit for covering an item you do not own.

## Plan structure

Choice and AHS may organize coverage into different plan tiers. Avoid comparing plan names. Compare actual systems, appliances, and components.

Create a table with one row per item and columns for Choice coverage, AHS coverage, limit, exclusion, service fee, and remedy. Mark unknowns and request written clarification.

## Heating and cooling

Review equipment types, quantities, compressors, coils, heat exchangers, controls, thermostats, refrigerant, line sets, ductwork, matching-system issues, permits, access, and code upgrades.

One provider may appear broader in a summary while using different component or limit language. Verify the complete agreement.

## Plumbing, electrical, and water heating

Compare pipe and wiring locations, stoppages, panels, outlets, fixtures, water heaters, pumps, excavation, opening walls, and restoration.

An approved repair may not include access or resulting property damage. Review these provisions for both providers.

## Appliances

Confirm refrigerators, additional refrigeration, dishwashers, disposals, ovens, ranges, cooktops, microwaves, washers, dryers, and specialty products.

Compare compressors, sealed systems, pumps, motors, bearings, tubs, drums, controls, sensors, glass, racks, doors, handles, hoses, and accessories. A listed appliance does not establish complete component coverage.

## Price

Record premium, enrollment charges, add-ons, billing term, service fee, and promotion. Convert all quotes to the same period.

Do not rely on a national starting price. Address, plan, date, payment schedule, and promotion can change the result.

## Service fee

Determine how each contract charges the fee: per request, visit, appliance, system, trade, or issue. Ask about denied diagnoses, repeat visits, callbacks, and contractor reassignment.

A lower fee can be paired with a higher premium. Compare total cost across several claim scenarios.

## Effective date

Record purchase, contract issue, effective, waiting-period, add-on, expiration, and renewal dates for both providers.

Review any exception tied to real-estate transactions or prior coverage. A waived waiting period does not necessarily cover a condition that already existed.

## Existing-condition language

Compare definitions of known, unknown, pre-existing, and detectable conditions. Keep inspection reports, maintenance records, prior invoices, and symptom dates.

Do not assume either provider covers a system already failing. If a claim is denied, request the technician's findings and contract provision.

## Maintenance and installation

Review both contracts for maintenance, improper installation, modifications, rust, corrosion, scale, sediment, pests, blocked airflow, drainage, utility supply, and unauthorized repair.

Use manufacturer instructions for maintenance. Documentation can clarify history but does not guarantee approval.

## Coverage limits

Create a limit table for every important item. Include per-component, per-item, per-event, annual, and aggregate limits.

Ask whether diagnosis, labor, parts, shipping, refrigerant, tax, access, and prior claims count toward the maximum. Compare usable benefits with current local repair and installed-replacement costs.

Do not declare one provider's limits better based on one headline amount.

## Contractor authorization

Learn how each provider accepts requests, selects technicians, and authorizes outside service. Verify local availability for the brands and system types at your property.

Ask:

1. What happens if no contractor accepts the job?
2. Can another provider be requested?
3. Is a second opinion available?
4. Who pays diagnosis after denial?
5. How are repeat visits handled?
6. Is emergency service offered?

Network size does not prove local availability or speed.

## Repair, replacement, and cash settlement

Compare who chooses the remedy and how value is calculated. Determine whether each provider may repair, replace, reimburse, or settle in cash.

Review delivery, haul-away, installation, refrigerant, ducts, lines, cords, hoses, trim, cabinets, permits, code upgrades, and taxes. Equipment approval does not necessarily cover the complete installation project.

## Add-ons

Compare only add-ons needed for the property. For each option, record premium, effective date, service fee, limit, components, exclusions, and local service.

A large add-on catalog is not automatically better. A single useful option with strong terms can matter more than several irrelevant choices.

## Cancellation and renewal

Compare cancellation windows, notice methods, administrative fees, claims deductions, and refund formulas. Review automatic renewal and how price changes are communicated.

At renewal, request updated agreements. Do not assume the first-term comparison remains valid.

## Customer reports

Reviews and complaints can identify questions about communication, scheduling, contractors, claim decisions, or cancellation. They represent different customers, contracts, locations, and periods.

Read detailed current reports from multiple sources. Use themes as verification questions rather than assigning an unsupported approval rate or overall winner.

## Scenario analysis

For both providers, test:

- No claims
- One covered appliance repair
- One covered system repair
- A denied diagnosis
- Multiple service requests
- A repair above the limit
- Unavailable parts
- Replacement or cash settlement
- No local contractor

Record premium, fees, uncovered cost, timing, control, and remedy.

## When Choice may fit

Choice may merit consideration when its current plan structure includes the items you need, its limits and fees fit your budget, and authorized local service is available.

That statement does not guarantee savings, approval, or timing.

## When AHS may fit

AHS may merit consideration when its current tiers, service-fee choices, and benefits align better with the property's systems and appliances.

That conclusion also depends on local service and current contract language.

## Self-funding alternative

A repair reserve preserves unused money and technician choice but leaves the household responsible for full cost and coordination. Both providers transfer only selected risks and retain contract restrictions.

Compare all three paths over the same period.

## Comparison worksheet

Record for each provider:

- Quote date and address
- Plan and premium
- Service fee
- Promotions
- Effective date
- Covered items and components
- Limits
- Exclusions
- Local service
- Repair and settlement method
- Cancellation and renewal

Attach the current agreements to the worksheet.

## Final decision

Choice and AHS should be treated as candidates until current quotes, contracts, and local service are compared. Select the plan that provides the most useful eligible protection for your equipment at an acceptable total cost.

Do not choose solely from a headline price, provider ranking, network claim, or complaint count.
