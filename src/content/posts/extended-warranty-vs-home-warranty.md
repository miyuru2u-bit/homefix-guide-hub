Extended warranty and home warranty sound alike and get confused constantly, but they solve different problems. Here's the fast side-by-side, then the real cost math on four typical scenarios.

## Extended warranty vs home warranty: at a glance

| Feature | Extended warranty | Home warranty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it covers | One specific appliance | Many appliances + often systems |
| Tied to | The product | The home |
| Appliance age | New only (at purchase) | Any age |
| Typical cost | $100 – $250 per appliance | $350 – $900 / year |
| Service fee per claim | Usually none | $75 – $150 |
| Length | 3–5 year term | Renews annually |
| Coverage starts | Immediately | After 30-day wait |
| Transferable | Sometimes | Yes (usually) |
| Best for | One new pricey unit | Multiple aging appliances |

## What is an extended warranty?

An **extended warranty** (also called an extended service plan or appliance protection plan) extends the coverage of the **manufacturer's warranty** on a **single product** — the dishwasher, refrigerator, or washer you just bought. It's typically offered by the retailer or manufacturer at the point of sale.

- **Covers:** mechanical and electrical defects/failures on that one appliance, beyond the manufacturer's standard period.
- **Tied to:** the specific product (and often its original owner).
- **Period:** a set number of years after purchase, usually 3–5.
- **Cost:** about **$100–$250** for a major appliance, varying by item and length.
- **Service fee:** usually none — covered repairs are fully paid.

It's essentially a way to keep "new appliance" protection going for a few more years on one item.

## What is a home warranty?

A **home warranty** is an annual service contract that covers the repair or replacement of **multiple appliances and/or systems** in your home when they break down from normal wear and tear — regardless of age or who bought them.

- **Covers:** many appliances (and often systems like HVAC, plumbing, electrical) under one plan.
- **Tied to:** your home, not a single product.
- **Period:** renews annually for as long as you keep paying.
- **Cost:** roughly **$350–$900/year** plus a **$75–$150 service fee** per claim.
- **Waiting period:** typically 30 days from sign-up before claims are payable.

It's designed for ongoing, whole-home peace of mind across aging appliances.

## What each one covers (and excludes)

### Extended warranty

**Covers:** defects and mechanical / electrical breakdowns on the covered appliance after the manufacturer warranty ends — full repair or replacement up to the unit's value.
**Excludes:** accidental damage, misuse, cosmetic parts, consequential damage (food loss, water damage), and acts of God. Coverage ends with the term.

### Home warranty

**Covers:** wear-and-tear breakdowns across many covered appliances (and systems on combo plans), for the service fee, up to per-item caps (commonly $1,000–$3,000 per item).
**Excludes:** pre-existing conditions, lack of maintenance, cosmetic parts, code violations, secondary damage, and items not named in the plan.

> 💡 **Neither is homeowners insurance.** Insurance covers sudden damage (fire, theft, certain water damage) — not mechanical breakdowns. Warranties and insurance complement each other; they don't replace one another.

## Real-world scenarios — which one wins?

### Scenario 1: One new $2,500 LG refrigerator

You just bought one premium fridge. Everything else in the kitchen is under 2 years old and still under manufacturer warranty.

- **Extended warranty:** ~$200 one-time for a 5-year extension.
- **Home warranty:** $600/year × 5 = $3,000 + service fees.

**Winner: extended warranty** by a wide margin. The home warranty would also cover other appliances, but they don't need it yet.

### Scenario 2: A 10-year-old house with original appliances

The fridge, dishwasher, washer, dryer, and oven are all original to the home. The HVAC is 12 years old.

- **Extended warranty:** not available — these are well past the new-purchase window.
- **Home warranty:** ~$650/year for a combo plan. One major HVAC or compressor repair ($1,500–$4,000) more than pays for the year.

**Winner: home warranty** — it's the only option that covers aging appliances and systems.

### Scenario 3: Newly bought home, mixed-age appliances

You just closed on a home. Some appliances are 2 years old, others are 8.

- **Extended warranty:** could cover only the newest items, leaving the older ones exposed.
- **Home warranty:** covers everything regardless of age, often with the waiting period waived at closing.

**Winner: home warranty** — especially because most providers waive the 30-day wait when bought through a real-estate transaction.

### Scenario 4: One $700 budget dishwasher

- **Extended warranty:** ~$120 for 4 years extra.
- **Out-of-pocket repair fund:** an average dishwasher repair is $150–$300; full replacement is $700.

**Winner: skip both** for this one item. The math rarely works on cheap appliances — self-insure with the money you would have spent.

## Cost comparison: which is cheaper?

The cheaper option flips depending on how many appliances you're protecting:

- **One new appliance:** extended warranty ($100–$250 one-time) is far cheaper than a home warranty ($350–$900/year).
- **A whole house of appliances:** buying separate extended warranties on each adds up fast — and only covers new ones. A single home warranty covers them all, including older units, for one annual fee.

The break-even is roughly **3+ aging appliances or 1 aging system** — at that point, a home warranty usually wins.

## When an extended warranty is better

- You just bought **one expensive appliance** (a high-end refrigerator or laundry pair).
- The rest of your appliances are new and still under manufacturer warranty.
- You want **no per-claim service fee** and protection tied to that specific unit.
- You don't want an ongoing annual commitment.

## When a home warranty is better

- You have **several appliances of varying ages**, including older ones.
- You want **whole-home** protection, possibly including systems (HVAC, plumbing, electrical).
- You'd rather pay a predictable annual fee and a small service fee than risk a big surprise repair.
- You're buying or selling a home (warranties are common in real-estate deals and often start at closing with no waiting period).

> ⚠️ **Don't double-pay.** Buying an extended warranty on an appliance already covered by your home warranty is wasted money. Map what's covered before adding more.

## Can you have both?

Yes — and sometimes it makes sense. For example, keep an extended warranty on a single luxury appliance (for its high replacement cost and no service fee) while carrying a home warranty for the rest of your aging appliances and systems. Just avoid overlapping coverage on the same item.

## How to decide in five steps

1. **Count your appliances and their ages.** Mostly new and few? Lean extended. Several and aging? Lean home warranty.
2. **Add up the alternative.** Compare the cost of separate extended warranties vs one home warranty over 3–5 years.
3. **Consider systems too.** If you also want HVAC / plumbing / electrical coverage, only a home warranty (combo plan) does that.
4. **Factor in service fees and caps.** Home warranties add per-claim fees and per-item caps; extended warranties usually don't.
5. **Check for overlap** with manufacturer warranties still in effect — don't pay for years you already have.

## Frequently asked questions

**What's the difference between an extended warranty and a home warranty?**
An extended warranty covers one specific appliance beyond its manufacturer warranty; a home warranty covers many appliances (and often systems) across your home for an annual fee, regardless of age.

**Is a home warranty better than an extended warranty?**
For multiple aging appliances, yes — it covers them all under one plan. For a single new, expensive appliance, an extended warranty is usually the cheaper, simpler choice.

**How much does an extended appliance warranty cost?**
About $100–$250 for a major appliance for a 3–5 year term. It's usually a one-time cost with no per-claim service fee.

**How much does a home warranty cost?**
Roughly $350–$900 per year plus a $75–$150 service fee per claim, depending on coverage and add-ons.

**Can I have both a home warranty and an extended warranty?**
Yes, but avoid covering the same appliance twice. Some homeowners keep an extended warranty on one luxury unit and a home warranty for everything else.

**Does either replace homeowners insurance?**
No. Insurance covers sudden damage like fire and certain water damage; warranties cover mechanical breakdowns. You generally want both.

## The bottom line

Extended warranties and home warranties solve different problems. An extended warranty is product-specific, cheap for one new appliance, and ends with its term. A home warranty is home-wide, covers appliances of any age (and often systems), and renews annually for a larger fee. If you're protecting one pricey new unit, go extended; if you're protecting a houseful of aging appliances, a home warranty usually wins on value. Count your appliances, compare the totals, and avoid paying twice for the same coverage.
