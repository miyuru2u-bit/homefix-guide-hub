An extended appliance warranty is generally a service contract beyond or alongside manufacturer protection. Its value depends on the written terms and the exact appliance.

## Eight checks before buying

1. **Overlap:** Does the plan run concurrently with manufacturer, retailer, or credit-card coverage?
2. **Scope:** Are diagnosis, labor, parts, travel, and in-home service included?
3. **Fees:** Is there a deductible, service fee, shipping charge, or repeat-visit fee?
4. **Exclusions:** Review installation, misuse, maintenance, cosmetic, surge, rust, corrosion, and accidental-damage language.
5. **Limits:** Check per-claim, aggregate, and replacement limits.
6. **Service:** Who selects the technician, and is local authorized service available?
7. **Remedy:** Who decides repair, replacement, reimbursement, or settlement?
8. **Exit:** Review cancellation, refund, transfer, and administrator-change provisions.

## Compare alternatives

Get the plan price in writing and compare it with setting the same amount aside for repair or replacement. Include likely installation, access, and disposal costs. Consider whether you would repair the appliance at all if a major failure occurred.

## Appliance complexity

Built-in installation, specialty parts, electronic controls, and limited local service can increase risk, but they do not prove a plan is good value. A restrictive limit or exclusion can neutralize that benefit.

> Do not rely on a checkout summary. Read and save the complete service contract before the return period expires.
