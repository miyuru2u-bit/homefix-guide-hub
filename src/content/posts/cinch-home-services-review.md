> ⚡ **Quick verdict:** Cinch Home Services (formerly **TotalProtect** and **HMS Home Warranty**) stands out for covering things many rivals won't — **certain rust and corrosion damage** and **unknown pre-existing conditions** — and for backing repairs with a long **180-day workmanship guarantee**. The trade-off is a higher **$100–$150 deductible** per visit. For homeowners who want broader coverage and repair peace of mind, Cinch is a strong, established choice despite the higher per-visit cost.

Cinch Home Services flies a little under the radar compared to the heavily advertised giants, but it's an established provider with a long history under its former TotalProtect and HMS brand names. Its pitch is broader coverage and a longer repair guarantee than most. This review breaks down Cinch's plans, pricing, its standout coverage quirks, and whether the higher deductible is worth it.

## Plans and pricing

Cinch offers three plans:

- **Appliances Plan** — covers major kitchen and laundry appliances.
- **Built-in Systems Plan** — covers HVAC, plumbing, electrical, water heater, and other systems.
- **Complete Home Plan** — combines both for full coverage. This is the recommended tier for most homeowners.

Cinch's **service fee (deductible)** runs about **$100–$150 per visit**, which is on the higher side. As with AHS's selectable fee, a higher deductible generally pairs with a lower monthly premium and vice versa, so you can lean the plan toward lower fixed cost or lower per-claim cost.

## The standout coverage quirks

Two things genuinely set Cinch apart:

**1. Rust and corrosion coverage.** Many home warranties flatly exclude damage caused by rust and corrosion. Cinch **covers some of it**, which can matter for older systems and components.

**2. Unknown pre-existing conditions.** This is rare. Most providers deny anything that predates your coverage. Cinch covers **certain unknown pre-existing conditions** — problems you couldn't have spotted with a visual inspection or simple test. (Known pre-existing issues are still excluded.) For buyers of older homes with uncertain histories, this is a meaningful advantage.

> 💡 **Tip:** Cinch's coverage of unknown pre-existing conditions makes it especially worth considering if you've just bought an older home and don't know the full history of its systems. Pair it with a home inspection — the inspection catches known issues, and Cinch's policy helps with the ones nobody could reasonably have found.

## The 180-day workmanship guarantee

Cinch backs covered repairs with a **180-day workmanship guarantee** — one of the longer guarantees in the industry. If the same problem recurs within 180 days of a covered repair, Cinch addresses it again **without charging another service fee**. That's real peace of mind and reduces the risk of paying repeatedly for a repair that didn't hold.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** The **$100–$150 deductible** is higher than budget providers' fees, so if you expect to file many small claims, the per-visit cost adds up. Run the math: Cinch's broader coverage and long guarantee are most valuable on **bigger, fewer** claims rather than frequent minor ones.

## Who Cinch Home Services is best for

Cinch is a good fit if you:

- Want **broader coverage** including some rust/corrosion and unknown pre-existing conditions.
- Value a **long 180-day workmanship guarantee**.
- Own an **older home** with an uncertain repair history.
- Don't mind a **higher deductible** in exchange for that coverage.

It's a weaker fit if you want the **lowest per-visit fee** or the absolute cheapest monthly premium (where Select leads).
