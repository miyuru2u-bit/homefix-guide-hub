Liberty Home Guard and American Home Shield sell service contracts for selected home systems, appliances, and optional property features. Their plan structures and marketing emphasis differ, but neither provider is universally better. The correct comparison uses current written quotes and agreements for the same address, date, coverage, billing period, and service-fee assumptions.

Do not decide from an add-on count, a national price, an online rating, or an unsupported approval statistic. Contract terms and local service determine practical value.

## Build one property inventory

Record the home's:

- Heating and cooling equipment
- Plumbing and electrical systems
- Water heaters and pumps
- Refrigerators and freezers
- Dishwashers and disposals
- Ovens, ranges, cooktops, and microwaves
- Washers and dryers
- Pools, spas, wells, septic, sump, or specialty equipment
- Additional units, detached structures, or rental use

For each item, note quantity, location, model, condition, known problems, and existing warranties. Use this same inventory for both providers.

## Obtain current documents

Collect a dated quote, complete state-specific agreement, add-on terms, promotion conditions, service-fee options, renewal language, and cancellation provisions from Liberty and AHS.

Plan names and benefits can change. Save the documents used for the decision instead of relying on a summary that may later be updated.

## Compare plan structure

Liberty may emphasize appliance, systems, combined, and optional coverage. AHS may use systems-focused and broader tiers. Compare actual covered items rather than the number or names of plans.

Create one row per item and columns for Liberty, AHS, limits, exclusions, effective dates, service process, and remedy.

## Add-ons

Compare only optional items the property actually needs. For each add-on, record:

1. Covered item and location
2. Component scope
3. Eligible causes
4. Premium
5. Service fee
6. Effective date
7. Limit
8. Exclusions
9. Local service
10. Repair or settlement method

A larger catalog is not automatically more useful. A single well-matched option can matter more than several irrelevant choices.

## Base systems coverage

For HVAC, compare equipment types, quantities, compressors, coils, heat exchangers, controls, thermostats, refrigerant, ductwork, matching issues, access, permits, and code upgrades.

For plumbing and electrical, review pipe and wiring locations, stoppages, panels, fixtures, pumps, water heaters, excavation, opening surfaces, and restoration. A broad category name does not establish component-level coverage.

## Appliance coverage

Confirm each refrigerator, freezer, dishwasher, disposal, oven, range, cooktop, microwave, washer, and dryer. Additional or specialty units may require optional coverage.

Compare sealed systems, compressors, pumps, motors, bearings, tubs, drums, controls, sensors, heating components, glass, racks, doors, handles, hoses, trim, and accessories.

## HVAC comparison

Do not declare one provider better for HVAC from a single maximum benefit. Review:

- Eligible equipment types
- Compressor and coil coverage
- Refrigerant and leak diagnosis
- Heat exchanger and combustion components
- Controls and thermostats
- Ductwork
- Matching-system requirements
- Obsolete equipment
- Electrical and drain work
- Permits and code upgrades
- Roof, attic, crawlspace, or crane access

Compare the usable benefit with complete local repair or replacement scope.

## Price and service fee

Request equivalent current quotes. Record premium, billing schedule, enrollment charges, service-fee choice, add-ons, promotions, and renewal terms.

Clarify whether the service fee applies per request, visit, appliance, system, trade, or issue. Ask about denied diagnoses, callbacks, repeat visits, and reassignment.

Run several scenarios rather than comparing the premium alone.

## Coverage limits

Identify per-component, per-item, per-system, per-event, annual, and aggregate limits. Add-ons may have separate maximums.

Ask whether diagnosis, labor, parts, shipping, refrigerant, tax, access, and previous claims reduce the limit. A high headline amount may provide less usable value after exclusions and accounting rules.

## Effective dates

Record purchase, issue, effective, waiting-period, add-on, expiration, and renewal dates. Obtain any exception in writing.

A newly purchased plan should not be assumed to cover a problem already present. Review existing-condition definitions for both providers.

## Existing conditions

Compare known, unknown, pre-existing, and detectable-condition language. Keep inspection reports, disclosures, maintenance records, and prior repair invoices.

If a claim is denied, request the written diagnosis, decision, and contract provision. Documentation can establish timing but does not guarantee approval.

## Maintenance and installation

Review exclusions for maintenance, improper installation, modification, blocked airflow, drainage, utility supply, rust, corrosion, scale, sediment, pests, and unauthorized repair.

Use manufacturer instructions for the exact equipment. Ask for written technician findings when maintenance or installation is cited in a decision.

## Contractor process

Learn how each provider receives service requests, selects contractors, and authorizes outside work. Verify service for the exact brands and specialties at the address.

Ask:

- What happens if no contractor accepts the request?
- Can another provider be assigned?
- Is outside service available with written approval?
- Is a second opinion allowed?
- Who pays diagnosis after denial?
- How are repeat visits handled?

Neither an add-on catalog nor network statement proves local response speed.

## Repair, replacement, and cash settlement

Compare who chooses the remedy and how value is calculated. Either agreement may allow repair, replacement, reimbursement, or cash settlement.

Review delivery, haul-away, installation, refrigerant, lines, ducts, cords, hoses, trim, panels, cabinets, permits, code work, and taxes. An approved product or component benefit may not cover the entire installed project.

## Specialty equipment

For pools and spas, inspect pumps, heaters, controls, cleaning equipment, salt systems, structures, and water-loss exclusions. For wells and septic systems, check pumps, tanks, lines, excavation, depth, access, and pumping.

For additional refrigeration, confirm quantity, location, environmental restrictions, sealed-system terms, and local technicians. These examples are comparison prompts, not statements that either plan includes the items.

## Customer reports

Ratings and complaints can surface questions about communication, scheduling, contractors, add-ons, claim interpretation, or cancellation. They reflect different contracts, dates, locations, and customer populations.

Use detailed recent reports as research leads. Do not turn an average rating or raw complaint count into a universal quality, satisfaction, or approval conclusion.

## Cancellation and renewal

Compare cancellation windows, notice methods, fees, claims deductions, refund formulas, and automatic renewal. Check whether add-ons can be removed separately.

Obtain new agreements at renewal. Pricing, limits, and options may change.

## Scenario analysis

Run identical scenarios for both providers:

1. No claims
2. One covered appliance repair
3. One covered system repair
4. One add-on claim
5. A denied diagnosis
6. Multiple service requests
7. A failure above the limit
8. Unavailable parts
9. Replacement or cash settlement
10. No local specialist

Record total cost, timing, control, and remedy.

## When Liberty may fit

Liberty may merit consideration when its current add-ons address eligible property features, the limits are useful, and local specialists are available.

That does not guarantee savings, approval, or faster service.

## When AHS may fit

AHS may merit consideration when its current plan tiers, service-fee options, systems and appliance benefits, and local network align better with the property.

That conclusion also requires current document review.

## Compare self-funding

A repair reserve preserves unused money and provider choice but leaves the household responsible for all costs and coordination. Both service contracts transfer only selected risks.

Compare Liberty, AHS, and self-funding over the same period.

## Comparison worksheet

Record:

- Quote date and address
- Plan and premium
- Service fee
- Add-ons
- Effective dates
- Covered components
- Limits
- Exclusions
- Local service
- Repair and settlement method
- Cancellation and renewal

Attach the contracts and mark unknown terms.

## Final decision

Select the provider whose current agreement covers the property's relevant items with meaningful limits, usable local service, and acceptable total cost. Neither Liberty nor AHS should be chosen solely from an add-on count, rating, price headline, or general winner claim.
