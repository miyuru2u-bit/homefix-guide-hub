Repair risk depends more on the failure than the appliance label. A common switch may be inexpensive on a premium product, while a major assembly can be costly on a basic model.

## High-cost signals

- Sealed refrigeration or refrigerant work
- Major tub, bearing, drum, or motor assemblies
- Control boards, displays, or induction modules
- Gas or high-voltage diagnosis
- Built-in, stacked, panel-ready, or inaccessible installation
- Proprietary or discontinued parts
- Multiple visits or long-distance travel
- Cabinet, utility, trim, permit, or code work

## Before approving a major repair

Request an itemized diagnosis and estimate. Confirm the exact part, availability, labor, return visits, warranty, and possibility of additional damage. Obtain another qualified opinion when the diagnosis is uncertain or the estimate approaches a suitable installed replacement.

## Warranty limits

A warranty may reduce eligible expense, but service fees, authorization rules, exclusions, limits, and settlement methods still apply. Do not equate “covered” with “fully paid.”
