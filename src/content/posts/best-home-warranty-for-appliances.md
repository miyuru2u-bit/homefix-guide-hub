## What separates a great appliance plan from a weak one

Before comparing brands, calibrate on what actually matters. A $35/month plan with a $500 cap on your $2,800 built-in fridge isn't a deal — it's a 20% discount you wait 6 weeks to collect.

1. **Per-item payout caps** — the dollar limit per claim. Common ranges: $500 (low, often Select basic), $1,500–$2,000 (typical), $3,000–$4,000 (AHS top tier). Match the cap to what your appliance would cost to replace.
2. **Covered-items list** — is your washer, dryer, built-in microwave, second fridge, ice maker, and stand-alone freezer explicitly named? Laundry is the #1 thing people assume is included that often isn't.
3. **Service fee** — flat $75–$150 per claim. Lower fee = higher premium. Pick based on how many claims you realistically expect.
4. **Workmanship guarantee** — if the repair fails again, do they re-fix it free? Industry norm is 30 days; Cinch leads at 180.
5. **Exclusions** — every contract excludes cosmetic parts, pre-existing conditions, and "lack of maintenance." The shorter and clearer this list, the better.
6. **Claims handling** — BBB rating, average response time, and whether they let you choose your own technician (AFC, Liberty) vs. assigning one (AHS, Choice).
7. **Waiting period** — usually 30 days from sign-up before you can claim. A few providers (Liberty Home Guard) offer 0-day options through real-estate channels.

## Provider deep-dives

### 1. American Home Shield — Best overall

**Plans:** ShieldSilver (systems only), ShieldGold (systems + appliances), ShieldPlatinum (everything + $1,500 roof, $1,000 HVAC tune-up, unlimited refrigerant).

**Appliance coverage on Gold/Platinum:** refrigerator, oven/range, dishwasher, built-in microwave, washer, dryer, garbage disposal, instant hot/cold dispenser.

**What stands out:** Per-item caps are the highest in the industry — up to **$4,000 on Platinum**, vs. an industry-average $2,000. AHS is also unusually willing to cover **undetectable pre-existing conditions** and units regardless of age, which most competitors exclude.

**Watch out for:** Premium runs $10–$20/month higher than budget players. Reviews flag occasional contractor-scheduling delays in metro areas. You don't get to pick the technician.

**Best for:** Owners of premium or built-in appliances (Sub-Zero, Wolf, Bosch), and anyone who wants the most generous payout caps available.

### 2. Select Home Warranty — Best on a budget

**Plans:** Bronze (appliances), Gold (systems), Platinum (combo + roof-leak).

**Appliance coverage:** Refrigerator, oven, stove, dishwasher, built-in microwave, garbage disposal. **Washer and dryer are an add-on** — confirm at sign-up.

**What stands out:** Among the lowest published premiums of any major provider. Free **roof-leak coverage** on every plan (most charge $5–$10/month extra). Frequent promotional discounts ($150 off + 2 months free is common).

**Watch out for:** Per-item caps are the lowest in this comparison — **$500 on basic appliances, up to $2,000 on Platinum**. That's a problem if your fridge cost $2,500 new.

**Best for:** Budget-conscious owners of standard mid-range appliances who want predictable monthly cost over maximum payout.

### 3. Liberty Home Guard — Most add-ons

**Plans:** Appliance Guard, Systems Guard, Total Home Guard.

**Appliance coverage:** Refrigerator, oven, range, dishwasher, washer, dryer, microwave, garbage disposal — plus an unusually deep add-on menu: second fridge, stand-alone freezer, ice maker, water dispenser, wine cooler, lighting fixtures, gutter cleaning, pest control.

**What stands out:** Consistently the **best customer-service ratings** among major providers (A rating on BBB, 4.7 stars on Trustpilot). 60-day workmanship guarantee. You can request a specific contractor.

**Watch out for:** Add-ons stack quickly — a fully loaded plan can hit $90/month. Caps are middle-of-the-pack at $2,000.

**Best for:** Appliance-heavy households (second kitchen, bar fridge, freezer in garage) and anyone who values responsive customer service over the cheapest premium.

### 4. Cinch Home Services — Best workmanship guarantee

**Plans:** Appliances, Built-in Systems, Complete Home.

**Appliance coverage:** Refrigerator, range, oven, cooktop, dishwasher, built-in microwave, washer, dryer, trash compactor.

**What stands out:** **180-day workmanship guarantee** — the longest in the industry. If the same problem comes back inside 6 months, they re-fix it at no charge and no second service fee. Also includes a $25 air-filter credit and a $25 water-filter credit annually.

**Watch out for:** Service fee runs higher at the top tier ($150). Not as many add-on options as Liberty.

**Best for:** Owners of aging appliances where the same part is likely to fail again (compressors, ice makers, drain pumps).

### 5. First American Home Warranty — Best starter plan

**Plans:** Starter, Essential, Premium.

**Appliance coverage:** Refrigerator, oven, range, cooktop, dishwasher, microwave, garbage disposal — washer/dryer are an add-on on Starter.

**What stands out:** Among the cheapest *combo* plans (appliances + HVAC) on the market. Covers **improper installations, code violations, and rust/corrosion** — issues most providers explicitly exclude, which matters for homes 25+ years old.

**Watch out for:** Limited geographic availability (not in all 50 states — check your zip first).

**Best for:** Owners of older homes with original-builder appliances and DIY-era electrical/plumbing.

### 6. AFC Home Club — Best long-term value

**Plans:** Silver (appliances), Gold (systems), Platinum (combo), Diamond (everything + electronics).

**What stands out:** **Lock in your rate for 3 years** — no other major provider does this. Also one of the only providers that lets you **choose your own licensed technician** (and pays them directly), which is huge if you already trust a local repair company.

**Watch out for:** 30-day waiting period, smaller national footprint, fewer reviews online to verify claims experience.

**Best for:** Homeowners who plan to stay 5+ years and want price certainty, or anyone with an existing repair tech they trust.

### 7. Choice Home Warranty — Simplest sign-up

**Plans:** Basic, Total. That's it — two tiers, no decision fatigue.

**What stands out:** **Flat $85 service fee**, no upsells, $3,000 cap per item, fastest online sign-up of any major provider (under 4 minutes). Frequent first-month-free promos.

**Watch out for:** Coverage is appliances-leaning — HVAC and electrical are weaker than the appliance side. Mixed reviews on claim denials related to "improper maintenance" — document everything.

**Best for:** First-time home warranty buyers who want clarity over customization.

### 8. 2-10 Home Buyers Warranty — Best for new construction

**Plans:** Simply Kept, Complete Home, Pinnacle Home.

**What stands out:** Originally a **structural-warranty company for builders** (10-year structural coverage), now also offers appliance/systems plans. Plans coordinate cleanly with builder warranties, and they have one of the strongest reputations for paying on systems claims.

**Watch out for:** Not the cheapest. Heavier focus on builder/realtor channel than direct-to-homeowner.

**Best for:** New construction (under 5 years old) and buyers in real-estate transactions.

**Total annual cost example** (typical 3-bedroom home, combo plan): $720 premium + 2 claims × $100 service fee = **$920/year**. One covered fridge compressor replacement alone runs $500–$1,200 out of pocket, so even a single major claim usually breaks even.

## How to choose the right plan for your home — 7-step framework

1. **List the appliances you actually need covered.** Walk your kitchen and laundry room. Note brand, age, and whether each is built-in or freestanding. Built-in units need higher caps.
2. **Decide: appliance-only or combo?** If your home is under 8 years old and your HVAC is new, appliance-only is fine. If your home is 10+ years old, the combo plan usually pays for itself in 1–2 HVAC claims.
3. **Match per-item caps to replacement cost.** If your fridge would cost $2,500 to replace, you need a $2,500+ cap. Cross off Select Basic.
4. **Pick a service-fee tier.** Expect 0–1 claims/year → take the $150 fee + lower premium. Expect 2+ → take the $75 fee.
5. **Read the exclusions list.** Common gaps: cosmetic parts, door seals, water filters, ice makers (often add-on), pre-existing conditions, lack-of-maintenance failures.
6. **Check claims reputation** on BBB and Trustpilot. Filter for reviews from the last 12 months only — older reviews reflect a different operator.
7. **Get 2–3 quotes** in your zip code. Same provider can vary $20+/month between states. Most providers offer first-month-free or $150-off promos.

> ⚠️ **The cheapest plan is rarely the best value.** A $35/month plan with $500 caps, 12-month exclusions, and a 30-day claims backlog will cost you more than a $55/month plan with $3,000 caps and a same-week response.

## Home warranty vs. other appliance protection

| Option | Covers | Typical cost | When it wins |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Home warranty** | Mechanical breakdown, multiple items | $400–$900/year | 3+ mid-life appliances |
| **Manufacturer warranty** | Defects on a new unit | Free (≈1 year) | First year of new appliance |
| **Extended warranty** | One specific unit, past mfr period | $100–$250 per item | Single high-value appliance |
| **Homeowners insurance** | Sudden damage (fire, flood) | Already paid | Disaster, not breakdown |

A home warranty is the only one of these that covers **multiple appliances simultaneously for mechanical breakdown** — which is exactly the failure mode most older appliances actually experience.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which home warranty company is best for appliances in 2026?**
American Home Shield is the most-cited overall pick for appliances thanks to high per-item caps ($3,000+) and broad coverage. Select Home Warranty wins on price, Liberty Home Guard on add-on flexibility, Cinch on its 180-day workmanship guarantee, and First American on affordable starter plans.

**How much does an appliance home warranty cost?**
Roughly $30–$75 per month plus a $75–$150 service fee per claim — about $400–$900 per year depending on coverage tier, add-ons, and your state.

**Does a home warranty cover the washer and dryer?**
Usually yes on the appliance or combo plan, but several providers (Select, First American) bundle them as an add-on instead of including them by default. Always confirm both are named in the covered-items list before signing.

**Are home warranties worth it for appliances?**
They typically pay off if you have 3+ mid-life appliances (5–15 years old). One covered compressor or motor replacement ($600–$1,500) can offset a full year of premiums. They make less sense for brand-new appliances still under manufacturer warranty.

**What's the difference between an appliance plan and a combo plan?**
Appliance plans cover only appliances (fridge, oven, dishwasher, washer/dryer). Combo plans add major systems (HVAC, plumbing, electrical). Combo runs $15–$25/month more but is usually the better value if your home is 10+ years old.

**What's the average per-item coverage cap?**
Most providers cap payouts at $1,500–$3,000 per appliance. AHS goes up to $4,000 on its top tier; Select caps at $500–$2,000 (low for premium built-ins). Always match the cap to your appliance's replacement cost.

**Can I get a home warranty on old appliances?**
Yes — unlike manufacturer warranties, home warranties cover appliances regardless of age, as long as they were working when you signed up. Pre-existing conditions and lack-of-maintenance failures are excluded.

**How long until coverage starts?**
Most providers enforce a 30-day waiting period from sign-up before you can file a claim. Some waive it if you're buying through a real-estate transaction.

**Do home warranties cover refrigerator compressors and washer motors?**
Yes — mechanical failure of compressors, motors, control boards, and heating elements is the core thing home warranties exist to cover. Cosmetic damage, water-line filters, and door seals are commonly excluded.

**Can I cancel a home warranty if I'm not happy?**
Yes. Most providers offer a 30-day money-back window if you haven't filed a claim, then prorated refunds after that minus a small admin fee. Read the cancellation clause before signing.

## The bottom line

The "best" home warranty for appliances isn't one company — it's the plan whose covered items, payout caps, service fee, and claims record match your home. **American Home Shield** wins on coverage breadth and the highest caps. **Select** wins on price (if you accept lower caps). **Liberty Home Guard** wins on add-ons and customer service. **Cinch** wins on the 180-day workmanship guarantee. **First American** wins on older-home coverage. **AFC** wins on long-term rate locks. **Choice** wins on simplicity. **2-10 HBW** wins on new construction.

Match the framework above to your home, get 2–3 quotes in your zip code, and you'll land on a plan that's actually worth the premium — not just cheap.
