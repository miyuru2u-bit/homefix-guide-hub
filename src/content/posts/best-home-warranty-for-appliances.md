A home warranty can turn a surprise $1,000 compressor failure into a $100 service call — but only if you pick a plan that actually covers your appliances the way you need. With dozens of providers and confusing plan tiers, "best" depends heavily on your situation. This guide breaks down what makes an appliance home warranty good, compares the top providers in 2026, and gives you a simple framework to choose.

## What makes a good appliance home warranty?

Before comparing brands, know what separates a strong appliance plan from a weak one:

- **The right covered items.** Confirm your refrigerator, oven/range, dishwasher, built-in microwave, and (if you want them) washer and dryer are named. Laundry is often an add-on.
- **High enough payout caps.** Per-item caps commonly run $1,000–$3,000. Higher caps matter if you own premium or built-in appliances.
- **A reasonable service fee.** Most plans let you trade a lower premium for a higher service fee (or vice versa). Pick the balance that fits how often you expect claims.
- **Few exclusions and clear contract language.** The fewer surprises in the fine print, the better.
- **Good claims reputation.** A cheap plan that denies claims or delays service isn't a bargain.
- **A workmanship guarantee.** Some providers re-fix a repair free within a set window (e.g., 30–180 days).

## Provider breakdown

### American Home Shield (AHS)

The biggest name in the industry and a frequent top pick for appliance coverage. AHS is known for broad coverage — including some older systems and undetectable pre-existing conditions other companies exclude — and notably high payout limits, with generous per-item caps. The trade-off is a premium that runs on the higher side and occasional reports of service delays. Best for homeowners who want maximum coverage breadth and high caps.

### Select Home Warranty

Consistently rated the budget choice, Select offers some of the lowest average monthly rates while still covering both appliances and systems. It includes roof-leak coverage in all plans (most charge extra) and keeps plan tiers simple. Best for cost-conscious buyers who still want solid coverage.

### Liberty Home Guard

Frequently praised for customer service and an unusually large menu of add-ons — letting you tailor coverage to extras like a second refrigerator, ice maker, or stand-alone freezer. Best for appliance-heavy households that want to customize.

### Cinch Home Services

Stands out for its **180-day workmanship guarantee** — if a covered repair fails again within that window, they fix it at no additional cost. Strong appliance coverage and a modern customer portal round it out. Best for homeowners nervous about repeat issues.

### First American Home Warranty

A solid value player with affordable starter plans and competitive basic appliance coverage. Some plans cover improper installations and code violations — a meaningful perk for older homes. Best for budget buyers who want a straightforward appliance plan.

## How much does an appliance home warranty cost?

Based on 2026 industry data:

- **Monthly premium:** roughly **$30–$75** for appliance-focused coverage (combo plans run higher).
- **Annual cost:** typically **$350–$900** depending on coverage and add-ons.
- **Service (call-out) fee:** **$50–$150** per claim, often chosen at sign-up.

Many providers let you lower the premium by accepting a higher service fee. If you expect few claims, a higher service fee saves money; if you expect several, a lower fee can pay off.

## How to choose the right plan for you

1. **List the appliances you actually need covered**, including washer/dryer and any second fridge or ice maker.
2. **Match the plan tier** — you usually need an appliance or combo plan, not a systems-only plan.
3. **Check the per-item caps** against the value of your appliances (premium/built-in units need higher caps).
4. **Pick a service-fee/premium balance** based on how many claims you expect.
5. **Read the exclusions** — cosmetic parts, pre-existing conditions, and maintenance-related failures are common gaps.
6. **Compare claims reputation and workmanship guarantees**, not just price.
7. **Get quotes from 2–3 providers** for an apples-to-apples comparison in your state.

> ⚠️ **Watch the gap between cheap and good.** The lowest premium isn't a deal if the plan has low caps, long exclusion lists, or a poor claims record. Weigh total value — premium + service fee + caps + reputation — not just the monthly price.

## Home warranty vs other coverage

A home warranty isn't the only way to protect appliances:

- **Manufacturer warranty** covers defects on new appliances (usually ~1 year) at no cost — making a home warranty partly redundant for brand-new units.
- **Extended/appliance warranty** covers a single item beyond the manufacturer period (~$150 for a major appliance).
- **Homeowners insurance** covers sudden damage (fire, certain water damage), not mechanical breakdowns.

A home warranty shines when you have **multiple mid-life appliances** and want predictable repair costs across all of them.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best home warranty for appliances?**
It depends on your needs, but American Home Shield (broad coverage/high caps), Select Home Warranty (budget), Liberty Home Guard (add-ons), Cinch (workmanship guarantee), and First American (affordable starter) are the most frequently recommended in 2026.

**How much does an appliance home warranty cost?**
Roughly $30–$75 per month plus a $50–$150 service fee per claim, or about $350–$900 per year depending on coverage and add-ons.

**Does a home warranty cover the washer and dryer?**
Often, but they're commonly an add-on rather than standard. Confirm both are named in the covered-items list.

**Are home warranties worth it for appliances?**
They can be, especially for homes with several mid-life appliances, where one covered major repair can offset much of a year's premium. For brand-new units under manufacturer warranty, the value is lower.

**What should I look for in an appliance home warranty?**
The right covered items, high enough payout caps, a reasonable service fee, few exclusions, a good claims reputation, and ideally a workmanship guarantee.

**Can I get a home warranty on old appliances?**
Yes — unlike a manufacturer warranty, home warranties cover items regardless of age, though pre-existing conditions are excluded.

## The bottom line

The "best" home warranty for appliances isn't a single brand — it's the plan whose covered items, caps, service fee, and claims record fit your home. American Home Shield leads on breadth and high caps, Select wins on budget, Liberty Home Guard on add-ons, Cinch on its workmanship guarantee, and First American on affordable starter coverage. Expect $30–$75/month plus a service fee, confirm your appliances are named, check the caps, and get a few quotes before deciding.
