Select Home Warranty markets service-contract plans for selected appliances, home systems, and combinations of both. Promotions can make a first-term quote appear attractive, but total cost, renewal terms, limits, exclusions, and local service determine practical value.

This review does not assign a universal ranking. Current terms can vary by address, state, enrollment channel, promotion, and renewal.

## Review method

Compare Select with alternatives using:

- Eligible property and occupancy
- Covered systems and appliances
- Component definitions
- Effective dates and waiting periods
- Premium, service fee, promotion, and renewal
- Limits and exclusions
- Contractor authorization and local availability
- Roof-related terms where offered
- Repair, replacement, and settlement
- Cancellation and transfer

Use a written quote and contract for the exact property.

## Plan structure

Select may offer appliance, systems, and combined options. Confirm current plan names and inclusions. List the home's HVAC, plumbing, electrical, water-heating, kitchen, and laundry equipment.

Mark which plan covers each item and whether optional coverage is required for pools, wells, septic equipment, additional refrigeration, or other features.

## Covered components

A plan category does not establish that every component qualifies. Review compressors, coils, heat exchangers, refrigerant, motors, pumps, controls, sensors, bearings, tubs, drums, sealed systems, glass, doors, racks, hoses, handles, and accessories.

Read coverage and exclusions together. Request written clarification if a summary conflicts with the agreement.

## Price and promotion

Obtain a dated quote showing premium, billing term, enrollment charges, service fee, add-ons, discounts, free months, and promotion conditions.

Ask:

1. When does the promotion end?
2. Does it require annual payment?
3. Is automatic renewal required?
4. Does the renewal price differ?
5. Can the discount be combined with other offers?
6. How does cancellation affect the promotion?

Compare the full first-term obligation, not only the advertised monthly equivalent.

## Renewal price

The initial quote may not state a future renewal amount. Review how renewal and price changes are handled and when notice is provided.

Set a reminder before renewal to obtain the next agreement and compare alternatives. A low introductory price does not guarantee low long-term cost.

## Service fee

Determine whether the fee applies per request, visit, appliance, system, trade, or issue. Ask about denied diagnoses, repeat visits, callbacks, and reassignment.

Run scenarios with no claim, one claim, and multiple claims. A lower premium paired with a higher service fee can produce different results depending on usage.

## Coverage limits

Find per-item, component, event, annual, and aggregate limits. Ask whether diagnosis, labor, parts, shipping, refrigerant, access, tax, and prior claims reduce the maximum.

Compare limits with current local repair and installed-replacement estimates. Do not assume a listed appliance or system will be paid in full.

## Roof-leak language

If promotional or optional roof-leak coverage is offered, review it separately. Confirm:

- Eligible roof types and materials
- Covered leak locations
- Effective date
- Inspection or maintenance requirements
- Per-event and annual limits
- Access and diagnosis
- Exclusions for structural damage, flashing, gutters, skylights, chimneys, or other features
- Whether interior or resulting damage is excluded
- Repair versus settlement method

Limited leak coverage is not the same as roof replacement, structural restoration, or homeowners insurance.

## Contractor process

Learn how to request service, who chooses the provider, and whether independent service can be authorized. Verify local availability for the home's brands and systems.

Ask what happens when no contractor accepts the request, whether a second opinion is available, and who pays diagnosis if coverage is denied. Do not infer response time from general marketing.

## Effective dates

Record purchase, issue, effective, waiting-period, add-on, expiration, and renewal dates. A promotion can affect price without changing eligibility timing.

A new plan should not be assumed to cover a system, appliance, or roof problem that already existed. Review definitions of known, unknown, pre-existing, and detectable conditions.

## Existing conditions

Keep inspection reports, seller disclosures, maintenance records, photographs, and prior repair invoices. These materials can establish timing but may also document a known defect.

If a claim is denied, request the diagnosis, decision, and cited contract provision in writing. Follow the appeal deadline.

## Maintenance and installation

Review exclusions for maintenance, improper installation, modification, blocked ventilation, drainage, utility supply, unauthorized repair, rust, corrosion, scale, sediment, and pests.

Use manufacturer manuals for equipment maintenance. For roofing, use qualified inspection and local requirements. Accurate records can clarify history but do not guarantee coverage.

## HVAC coverage

Check heating and cooling separately. Review equipment types, compressors, coils, heat exchangers, controls, thermostats, refrigerant, ductwork, permits, code upgrades, and matching-system issues.

A budget-oriented premium may be less relevant if the HVAC limit or exclusions do not address the home's actual equipment.

## Appliance coverage

Confirm refrigerators, dishwashers, cooking appliances, microwaves, washers, dryers, and additional units. Check component limits and exclusions.

For built-in, panel-ready, or stacked products, determine whether removal and reinstallation are covered.

## Plumbing and electrical

Review pipe and wiring locations, stoppages, fixtures, panels, water heaters, pumps, access, excavation, code work, and restoration.

An approved component repair may not include opening and restoring walls, floors, ceilings, or concrete.

## Repair, replacement, and cash settlement

The administrator may choose repair, replacement, reimbursement, or cash. Review valuation and whether delivery, haul-away, installation, utilities, permits, code upgrades, trim, cabinets, and tax are included.

A cash offer can be below the complete installed cost while still following a contract formula. Understand the formula before purchasing.

## Cancellation and refund

Check the cancellation period, required notice, fees, claims deductions, and refund method. Promotions and free months can affect the calculation.

Retain written cancellation confirmation and monitor automatic payments.

## Customer feedback

Recent detailed reviews can surface questions about communication, scheduling, contractor quality, promotions, roof claims, or cancellation. Ratings change and can reflect different locations and contracts.

Use them as research leads, not proof of a universal claim outcome or quality score.

## Scenario analysis

Model:

- No claim
- One covered appliance repair
- One covered system repair
- A denied diagnosis
- Several service requests
- A repair above the limit
- A roof leak claim
- Replacement or cash settlement
- No local contractor

Record total cost, timing, control, and likely remedy from the agreement.

## Who may consider Select

Select may be worth comparing when the current quote is competitive, relevant items and components are covered, limits are meaningful, and local service is available.

A promotion alone does not establish value.

## Who may prefer another option

Another plan or self-funding may fit when important limits are too low, roof terms are narrow, renewal cost is unclear, contractor access is unsuitable, or the household wants unrestricted provider choice.

## Final assessment

Select Home Warranty may be a candidate for a budget-focused comparison, but the decision should use total cost and the current agreement. Verify promotions, renewal, service fees, limits, roof language, local service, exclusions, and settlement terms.
