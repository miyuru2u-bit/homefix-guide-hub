First American Home Warranty and American Home Shield both offer service contracts for selected systems and appliances. Neither provider is automatically better, cheaper, or more suitable for an older property. Compare current state-specific agreements and written quotes for the same address and coverage.

If the plan is connected to a home sale, obtain the applicable buyer, seller, or real-estate agreement. Those terms may differ from direct-to-consumer coverage.

## Build one property inventory

Record the home's HVAC, plumbing, electrical, water-heating, kitchen, and laundry equipment. Include model numbers, quantities, locations, condition, known issues, prior repairs, and manufacturer coverage.

Use the same inventory for both providers. Do not compare generic plan names.

## Obtain current documents

Collect from both companies:

- Dated quote
- Full state-specific agreement
- Plan and add-on description
- Service-fee options
- Promotion conditions
- Effective-date information
- Renewal language
- Cancellation and transfer provisions

Save copies. Product terms can change after an article is published.

## Compare plan structure

First American and AHS may group systems and appliances differently. Match equivalent coverage based on actual items and components.

Create one row per item and columns for First American, AHS, limit, exclusions, service process, and remedy. Mark unknowns instead of assuming a benefit.

## Appliance coverage

Review refrigerators, freezers, dishwashers, disposals, ovens, ranges, cooktops, microwaves, washers, dryers, and additional units.

Compare compressors, sealed systems, pumps, motors, bearings, tubs, drums, controls, sensors, glass, racks, doors, handles, hoses, and accessories. A listed appliance may still have component-level exclusions.

## Systems coverage

For HVAC, inspect equipment types, compressors, coils, heat exchangers, refrigerant, controls, thermostats, ductwork, matching-system issues, permits, and code upgrades.

For plumbing, electrical, and water heating, compare pipe and wiring locations, stoppages, panels, fixtures, pumps, access, excavation, and restoration.

An approved system repair may not include every surrounding installation expense.

## Price and service fee

Request current quotes for equivalent coverage. Record premium, billing schedule, enrollment charges, add-ons, service fee, promotion, and renewal terms.

Clarify whether the fee applies per request, visit, appliance, system, trade, or issue. Ask about denied diagnoses, callbacks, return visits, and reassignment.

A lower premium can be paired with a higher fee or narrower coverage. Compare total cost.

## Coverage limits

Check per-component, per-item, per-system, per-event, annual, and aggregate limits. Do not interpret “unlimited claims” as unlimited financial benefit unless the agreement explicitly supports that conclusion.

Ask whether diagnosis, labor, parts, shipping, refrigerant, tax, access, and previous claims reduce the maximum. Compare usable benefits with local repair and installed-replacement costs.

## Older systems and appliances

Age alone may not establish eligibility. Compare how both agreements address existing conditions, maintenance, installation, rust, corrosion, obsolete parts, and settlement value.

Older equipment can be difficult to repair when parts are unavailable or incompatible. Review what each provider may do when a part cannot be obtained.

## Existing-condition language

Compare definitions of known, unknown, pre-existing, and detectable conditions. Keep inspection reports, seller disclosures, maintenance history, and prior service invoices.

A new plan should not be assumed to cover an item already malfunctioning. If coverage is denied, request the technician report and cited provision.

## Maintenance and installation

Review each contract for maintenance, improper installation, modification, blocked airflow, drainage, utility supply, rust, corrosion, scale, sediment, pests, and unauthorized repair.

Use manufacturer instructions for equipment maintenance. Documentation can establish history but cannot guarantee approval.

## Effective dates and waiting periods

Record purchase, contract issue, effective, waiting-period, add-on, expiration, and renewal dates. Real-estate plans may use closing, listing, transfer, or another event.

Obtain any exception or waiver in writing. A waived waiting period does not necessarily cover a pre-existing failure.

## Real-estate coverage

If a buyer, seller, agent, or closing party is purchasing the plan, identify:

1. Which agreement applies
2. Who is the contract holder
3. When coverage begins
4. Which options were selected
5. Who pays service fees
6. Whether coverage transfers at closing
7. What happens if closing is delayed or canceled

Do not assume real-estate coverage automatically starts or transfers.

## Contractor process

Learn how each provider accepts service requests, selects contractors, and authorizes outside work. Verify local providers for the property's brands and system types.

Ask what happens if no contractor accepts the job, whether another provider can be requested, whether a second opinion is available, and who pays diagnosis after denial.

Neither provider's national footprint guarantees local service speed.

## Repair, replacement, and cash settlement

Compare who chooses the remedy and how value is calculated. The agreement may allow repair, replacement, reimbursement, or cash settlement.

Review delivery, haul-away, installation, refrigerant, ducts, lines, cords, hoses, trim, panels, cabinets, permits, code upgrades, and taxes. A replacement benefit may not cover the complete installed project.

## Add-ons

Compare only options relevant to the property. For each, record premium, effective date, service fee, limit, component scope, exclusions, and local service.

Do not assume a larger catalog or lower add-on price provides more useful protection.

## Cancellation, transfer, and renewal

Compare cancellation windows, notice methods, fees, claims deductions, and refund formulas. Review automatic renewal and price-change notices.

For a home sale, confirm transfer rules, forms, deadlines, and fees. At renewal, obtain new agreements and repeat the analysis.

## Customer reports

Reviews and complaints can reveal questions about communication, contractors, claim decisions, real-estate administration, or cancellation. They reflect different states, contracts, properties, and dates.

Use detailed current reports as research leads rather than proof of a winner or likely approval.

## Scenario analysis

Run identical scenarios:

- No claims
- One covered appliance repair
- One covered systems repair
- A denied diagnosis
- Several service requests
- A failure above the limit
- Obsolete parts
- Replacement or cash settlement
- No local contractor
- Property sale or transfer

Record total cost, timing, control, and remedy.

## When First American may fit

First American may merit consideration when its current appliance and systems provisions, service process, and real-estate agreement align with the property.

This does not guarantee savings, approval, or transfer.

## When AHS may fit

AHS may merit consideration when its current plan tiers, service-fee options, limits, and coverage provisions better match the property.

That conclusion also requires current local-service verification.

## Compare self-funding

A repair reserve preserves unused money and technician choice but leaves the owner responsible for full costs and coordination. Both contracts transfer selected risks only.

Compare all options over the same period.

## Comparison worksheet

Record:

- Quote date and address
- Plan and premium
- Service fee
- Promotion
- Effective date
- Covered items
- Limits
- Exclusions
- Real-estate terms
- Local service
- Repair and settlement method
- Cancellation, transfer, and renewal

Attach both agreements.

## Final decision

Compare First American and AHS from current documents, not a generalized winner. Select the contract that provides useful eligible protection for the property's actual systems and appliances at an acceptable total cost.
