> ⚡ **Quick verdict:** American Home Shield's waiting period is **30 days** from your contract's effective date across every plan tier. It's **waived at closing** for real-estate transactions and for **existing customers who renew** without a gap. Filing during the wait = automatic denial, so pick your start date carefully.

The waiting period is the single most misunderstood part of buying an AHS plan. New customers regularly get a claim denied in week two because they didn't realize coverage hadn't kicked in yet. Here's the full picture, above the fold, followed by the exceptions and how to avoid a denial.

## What the waiting period actually is

The waiting period is a **30-day window between the contract's effective date and the day you can file a claim**. It exists for one reason: to stop people from buying a plan the day a system breaks and expecting the warranty to pay for it. AHS treats any breakdown reported inside that window as a **pre-existing condition** — even if you didn't know about it — and denies the claim.

The clock starts on your **contract effective date**, which is usually the date payment clears, not the date you signed up. On day 31 the plan is fully active.

## What's covered before the wait ends

Nothing. The only exceptions are the ones that skip the wait entirely (below). During the 30-day window:

- You **can** call AHS to set up your account, register appliances, and confirm coverage details.
- You **cannot** file a covered service request.
- You **cannot** back-date a breakdown that happened before the effective date.
- Your **monthly premium still charges** during the wait — you're paying for future coverage.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** A technician dispatched during the waiting period will still collect the ~$100–$125 trade-call fee, and AHS won't refund it when the claim is denied. If something breaks in month one, use an independent repair service and eat the cost — don't burn the fee.

## When the waiting period is waived

Two situations skip the wait entirely:

### 1. Real-estate transactions

If the plan is purchased as part of a **home sale** — whether the buyer pays or the seller includes it as a closing gift — coverage starts **at closing** with no waiting period. This is the single biggest reason home warranties are common in real-estate contracts: buyers get immediate protection on inherited systems and appliances.

Sellers who list their home can also get **listing-period coverage** on major systems while the house is on the market, again with no wait.

### 2. Continuous existing customers

If you're already an AHS customer and you **renew before the current contract expires**, there's no new waiting period — coverage stays continuous. Same rule applies if you **upgrade** from Shield Silver to Complete or Platinum mid-contract: your existing covered items stay covered immediately, and only *newly added* categories (if any) carry a fresh 30-day wait.

Let your contract lapse for even one day and the 30-day clock resets when you re-enroll.

## How to avoid a denied claim in the first month

- **Time the effective date around known upcoming issues.** If your AC is limping into summer, don't buy on May 20 expecting a June 1 fix.
- **Document current condition at signup.** Take dated photos and keep service receipts. It's the single best defense against a "pre-existing condition" denial after day 31 too.
- **Use the real-estate waiver when eligible.** If you're closing on a house, roll the plan into the transaction — you get coverage on day one instead of day 31.
- **Renew before expiration.** A single-day lapse resets the 30-day clock on your next contract.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you're buying AHS outside a real-estate transaction and your systems are aging, budget for one repair out-of-pocket during the waiting period. That's the realistic cost of onboarding, and it usually still beats going uninsured.
