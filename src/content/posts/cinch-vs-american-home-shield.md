Cinch Home Services and American Home Shield sell service contracts for selected home systems and appliances. Their marketing may emphasize different benefits, but the agreement issued for the property controls eligibility, service, limits, and remedies. Neither company is universally better or cheaper.

Compare current written quotes for the same address, date, systems, appliances, optional coverage, billing term, and service-fee assumptions.

## Build one property inventory

Record the home's heating, cooling, plumbing, electrical, water-heating, kitchen, and laundry equipment. Include full model numbers, quantities, locations, condition, known issues, prior repairs, and existing warranties.

Use the same inventory for both providers. Do not give either plan credit for covering an item you do not own or omit a relevant second unit.

## Obtain current documents

Save a dated quote and complete state-specific agreement from each provider. Also save add-on terms, promotion conditions, service-fee choices, renewal language, and cancellation provisions.

Plan names can stay similar while details change. A historical comparison may not describe the contract available today.

## Compare plan structure

Cinch may organize coverage into appliance, systems, and combined options. AHS may use systems-focused and broader tiers. Do not compare names or number of plans. Compare the covered items and components in the two options that best match your inventory.

Create a table with one row per system or appliance and columns for Cinch coverage, AHS coverage, limits, exclusions, and service rules.

## Workmanship and re-service

Cinch and AHS may describe workmanship, callback, or re-service terms differently. Verify:

1. How long the applicable period lasts
2. When it begins
3. Whether it covers labor, parts, or both
4. Whether the same failure and contractor are required
5. Whether another service fee applies
6. What happens after cancellation or expiration
7. How a recurring symptom must be reported

Do not interpret a headline workmanship promise as unlimited coverage for every later failure.

## Existing and detectable conditions

Compare the definitions of known, unknown, pre-existing, and detectable conditions. If Cinch materials discuss undetectable conditions, identify the exact clause, eligibility requirements, and exclusions. Apply the same review to AHS.

Keep inspection reports, seller disclosures, maintenance records, and prior repair invoices. These documents can establish timing but do not guarantee approval.

## Rust and corrosion

Review how each agreement treats rust, corrosion, scale, sediment, deterioration, and related leakage. Determine whether the language varies by item or component and whether cosmetic or structural damage is treated separately.

A general statement about corrosion should not be read as coverage for every corrosion-related failure, access cost, or resulting loss.

## HVAC coverage

Compare heating and cooling separately. Check equipment types, quantities, compressors, coils, heat exchangers, blower components, controls, thermostats, refrigerant, line sets, ductwork, matching-system issues, permits, and code upgrades.

Ask how each provider treats obsolete refrigerant and unavailable compatible equipment. Compare coverage for the failed component and related installation work.

## Plumbing and electrical coverage

Review pipes, drains, stoppages, fixtures, water heaters, pumps, panels, wiring, switches, outlets, access, excavation, and restoration.

An approved repair may not include opening walls, floors, ceilings, or concrete. Compare access and restoration provisions directly.

## Appliance coverage

Confirm each refrigerator, freezer, dishwasher, disposal, oven, range, cooktop, microwave, washer, and dryer. Additional or specialty units may require an add-on.

Review compressors, sealed systems, pumps, motors, bearings, tubs, drums, controls, sensors, glass, racks, doors, handles, hoses, and accessories. A listed appliance may still contain excluded components.

## Price and service fee

Request current quotes. Record premium, billing schedule, enrollment charges, service-fee options, add-ons, promotions, and renewal language.

Clarify whether the fee applies per request, visit, appliance, system, trade, or issue. Ask about denied diagnoses, repeat visits, callbacks, and reassignment.

Test no-claim, one-claim, and multiple-claim scenarios. Do not compare premium alone.

## Coverage limits

Create a table of per-component, per-item, per-event, annual, and aggregate limits. Ask which costs reduce each maximum, including diagnosis, labor, parts, shipping, refrigerant, tax, access, and previous claims.

Compare usable benefits with current local repair and complete installed-replacement estimates for the actual equipment.

## Exclusions

Compare maintenance, improper installation, modifications, misuse, commercial use, unauthorized repair, rust, corrosion, scale, sediment, pests, power events, weather, water damage, cosmetic items, and secondary damage.

These are categories to investigate, not a claim that every agreement uses identical language.

## Contractor authorization

Learn how each company accepts service requests, chooses contractors, and authorizes outside work. Verify provider availability for the address, brands, and equipment types.

Ask:

- What happens if no contractor accepts the job?
- Can another provider be requested?
- Is a second opinion available?
- Who pays diagnosis after denial?
- How are callbacks handled?
- Is emergency service available?

Do not infer service speed from general network statements.

## Repair and replacement

Compare who chooses repair, replacement, reimbursement, or cash settlement and how value is calculated.

Review delivery, haul-away, installation, refrigerant, ducts, lines, cords, hoses, trim, cabinets, permits, code work, and taxes. A replacement approval may not pay the complete installed project.

## Effective dates

Record purchase, issue, effective, waiting-period, add-on, expiration, and renewal dates. A timing exception should be confirmed in writing.

Neither plan should be assumed to cover a problem that already began. Document symptoms and dates honestly.

## Add-ons

Compare only optional items relevant to the property. For each, record premium, effective date, limit, service fee, component scope, exclusions, local service, and remedy.

A larger add-on catalog is not automatically better. One well-matched benefit can be more useful than several irrelevant options.

## Cancellation and renewal

Review cancellation timing, fees, claims deductions, refund formula, automatic renewal, and notice requirements. Promotions may not continue.

At renewal, obtain new agreements and repeat the comparison. Do not assume today's conclusion remains valid.

## Customer feedback

Detailed reviews can highlight questions about communication, contractor scheduling, claim interpretation, workmanship, or cancellation. Ratings and complaint counts reflect different customer populations, dates, locations, and contracts.

Use those reports to build verification questions. Do not convert them into an unsupported approval rate or winner.

## Scenario analysis

Run the same scenarios for both providers:

1. No claims
2. One covered appliance repair
3. One covered systems repair
4. A denied diagnosis
5. A recurring symptom
6. Multiple service requests
7. A failure above the limit
8. Unavailable parts
9. Replacement or cash settlement
10. No local contractor

Record total cost, timing, provider control, and likely remedy.

## When Cinch may fit

Cinch may merit consideration when its current plan structure, workmanship terms, and condition language match the property and local service is available.

That does not guarantee savings, approval, or response time.

## When AHS may fit

AHS may merit consideration when its current tiers, service-fee options, limits, and coverage provisions align better with the property's systems and appliances.

That conclusion also requires local service verification.

## Compare self-funding

A repair reserve preserves unused money and provider choice but leaves the household responsible for the full cost and service coordination. Either contract transfers only selected risks.

Compare Cinch, AHS, and self-funding over the same period and property inventory.

## Final comparison

Select only after verifying current premium, service fee, covered components, limits, workmanship or re-service terms, existing-condition and corrosion language, local contractors, and replacement provisions.

Neither Cinch nor AHS is universally superior. The stronger fit is the contract that addresses the property's actual risks at an acceptable total cost.
