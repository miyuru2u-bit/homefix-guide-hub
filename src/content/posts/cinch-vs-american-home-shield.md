Cinch and American Home Shield offer service-contract coverage for home systems and appliances. Their marketing emphasizes different benefits, but the contract controls each claim.

## What to compare

- Premium and service-fee options
- Covered components in equivalent tiers
- Item-level and aggregate payout limits
- Workmanship and repeat-service conditions
- Detectable or pre-existing-condition definitions
- Rust, corrosion, code, access, and modification exclusions
- Contractor authorization and cash-out rules

## Decision approach

Cinch may be attractive when its current workmanship and condition language fits an older property. AHS may be attractive when its available plan structure and limits fit expensive systems. Treat both as candidates until you compare state-specific documents and local service availability.

Do not assume that a longer advertised guarantee applies to every repeat visit, or that a covered condition overrides exclusions elsewhere in the agreement.

> Verify current pricing, availability, limits, and contract language directly with both providers.
